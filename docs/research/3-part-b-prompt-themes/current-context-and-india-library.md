# Current Context (2025–26) and Indian Everyday-Life Library for CEED Part B Prompts

Scope: CEED 2027 (17 Jan 2027). Paper likely set mid-to-late 2026, so hooks are drawn from roughly mid-2025 to late 2026. Part B slots used below (per CEED 2027 brochure and past papers, see `../1-exam-and-prep/exam-structure.md`):

| Code | Slot | Typical form in past papers |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Sketching | Scene in perspective / unusual POV (ceiling-camera selfie 2023; kitchen from a chair's POV 2026) |
| Q2 | Creativity | Many ideas, alternative uses, storyboard, narrative |
| Q3 | Visual sensitivity | Icon set, logo, poster, pictograms (zoo animals, science museum, women's hockey logo) |
| Q4 | Form sensitivity | Product/form exploration, 3D form, material/structure |
| Q5 | Problem identification | Scene → list and sketch problems for users (rural kitchen 2024, vegetable cart 2025, elderly couple Srinagar→Chennai 2026) |

Pattern visible in past Q5 prompts: a **specific user group + a specific everyday setting + a journey or transaction**. Current-context hooks are usually folded in lightly (e.g. an elderly couple on a long train journey) rather than named as news events. Treat the news below as *setting flavour*, not as the question itself.

## Part A — Current context 2025–2026: which events/trends are likely design-question hooks?

### Takeaway
The strongest 2025–26 hooks for a CEED setter are those that (a) touch everyday Indian life, (b) are visually drawable, and (c) are non-partisan: extreme heat and floods, ageing/eldercare on journeys, new rail (Vande Bharat Sleeper), the first digital Census, UPI-everywhere payments, gig/delivery workers, women's sport (World Cup win, chess), space (Shubhanshu Shukla, Gaganyaan), and mega-gatherings (Kumbh). Disasters and tragedies (Kumbh crush, Air India crash) are more likely to appear indirectly as "crowd safety" or "emergency wayfinding" than named.

**Patterns for the prompt generator**
- CEED rarely names a news event. It converts trends into **user + setting + moment** (e.g. ageing → "elderly couple on a long train journey"). Generator should do the same: pick a trend, then render it as a scene.
- Q3 visual-sensitivity prompts often name a real-sounding Indian institution (Indian Women's Hockey Association, a zoo, a science museum). Fit: "Women's cricket academy", "CWG 2030 sport pictograms", "Gaganyaan mission patch", "AQI scale", "Census self-enumeration icons".
- Q5 prompts in 2024–26 moved geographically: rural kitchen (unspecified) → urban vegetable cart → pan-India journey (Kashmir to Tamil Nadu). Expect regional specificity; generator should rotate regions.
- Avoid communally or politically charged framings (religious conflict, elections, protests as protests). Festivals are fine as everyday life; ASHA strikes become "ASHA's day", not "ASHA protest".

## Part B — Indian everyday context library: settings, users, objects, rituals, crafts, pain points

### Takeaway
A usable library for prompt generation is a matrix of **setting × user × object × moment**, rotated across regions (N/S/E/W/NE, hill/coast/desert/plains) and class (slum, chawl, middle-class flat, rural kutcha/pucca house, gated society). Every past CEED Q5 fits this matrix. Below is a structured bank; entries are general cultural knowledge rather than individually cited facts.

#### B7. Regional diversity checklist (for generator rotation)
- **Geography:** Himalayan hill town (Shimla, Gangtok), desert (Jaisalmer), coastal (Kochi, Puri), riverine/flood plain (Assam, Bihar), Deccan plateau (Hyderabad), island (Andaman), tribal forest (Bastar, Jharkhand).
- **Climate moment:** May heat, July monsoon, December fog/smog, Kashmir winter.
- **Settlement scale:** Metro, tier-2 city, small town, village, nomadic/pastoral (Gaddi, Rabari, Bakarwal).
- **Language/script:** Signage tasks can specify bilingual/trilingual (e.g. Tamil + English, Hindi + Urdu + English).
- **Class:** Avoid defaulting to middle-class urban; past CEED prompts lean toward ordinary/low-income users (cart vendors, rural kitchen).
