# Design History Knowledge for CEED (content bank)

Scope: art/design history and design-knowledge items for CEED Part A flashcards and Part B design-reference prompts. Evidence base: all official CEED question papers 2015–2026 on the CEED site (2016 had two papers), plus official answer keys for 2020–2026, downloaded to `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-r1ckrck-Documents-Projects/10fb3a1a-27c2-482d-8f98-14019155dbff/scratchpad/ceed-history/`. The papers' index is on the [CEED 2027 home page](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/) (links `downloads/CEED20xxqp.pdf`, `CEED_20xx_Question_Paper.pdf`).

Priority legend used in every table:
- **P1**: this item or category appeared in an official paper (year/Q given).
- **P2**: sits next to a P1 category (same question format, different answer). These are the likely re-tests.
- **P3**: core canon with no paper evidence yet.

## Q1. Which art/design history items actually appeared in CEED Part A (2015–2026)?

### Takeaway
GK questions were common in 2015–2019 (painters, films, logos, fonts, movements, Indian sculpture and architecture, festivals). Since 2020 they have shrunk to about 4–7 per paper, all image-based. The formats stay stable: **identify or order images** (art movements, monuments, dance forms, Indian painting styles), **traditional craft process** (wooden or metal artefact operations, asked 2019, 2021, 2025, 2026), **ergonomic percentile choice** (2022, 2026), **Gestalt** (2026), **colour scheme** (2023), **UI component correctness** (2026). Chronological ordering of movements was asked in 2015 and 2018; image-to-movement identification in 2026.

### Inferences
- **Recurring frequency (2015–2026)**: painter/movement ID or ordering 7 times; Indian painting/sculpture/architecture/monuments 6; logos 6; craft-process operations 4 (2019, 2021, 2025, 2026); dance 2; ergonomics percentile/height 3; Gestalt 1 (new in 2026); colour scheme 1; UI components 1 (new in 2026). Font-matching appears in nearly every paper, but it is visual reasoning, not history.
- **Expected for 2027**: 1 art-movement image item; 1 Indian culture ordering item (monuments, dance, paintings, crafts by state, all asked "in sequence"); 1 craft-process item; 1 ergonomics percentile item; 1 design-principle item (Gestalt, colour, or UI). Total history/GK load is about 5 of 44 questions, roughly 15–20 marks of 150.
- **The question shape is "sequence of N images → pick the option"**. Flashcards should therefore be image-first, and the app should generate multi-item ordering drills, not single-term recall.
- Since 2020 the papers have not asked pure trivia (films, authors, inventors). Low priority for the bank.

### Gaps
- No official answer keys online for 2015–2019; the answers marked "inferred" are mine.
- UCEED past papers are not linked on the UCEED 2027 site (only the brochure: [UCEED 2027](https://www.uceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/)). UCEED not mined.
- 2010–2014 CEED papers are linked on the site but were not mined.
- Coaching-institute analyses were not consulted. The official papers were enough and are more reliable.
- Image-only options (e.g. 2015 Q11, 2016 Picasso/Rembrandt/Klimt paintings, 2019 Q35 products) were not viewed individually.

## Q2. Core global canon: movements, designers, iconic objects

### Takeaway
Prioritise the movements CEED has already shown: De Stijl, Impressionism, Pop Art, Baroque, Art Nouveau, Renaissance, Realism, Cubism, Arts & Crafts, Art Deco. Then add the design-specific modern movements (Bauhaus, Swiss Style, Memphis, Postmodernism), which fit the "design knowledge" syllabus line. Each card needs: dates, one-line visual signature, 2–3 key names, and the single most-reproduced image (the setters pick famous exemplars).

### Inferences
- The 2026 Q28 toy-furniture match used famous contemporary furniture types without naming designers: an inflatable chair, stacked corrugated-cardboard chairs, a spinning-top chair. ### Gaps

## Q3. Indian design history, crafts, culture

### Takeaway
Indian content is the most consistently tested GK cluster: painting styles, dance, monuments, crafts by state, traditional craft processes, Raja Ravi Varma, sculpture schools, architecture styles. Indian *design-institution* history (NID, IDC, Eames India Report) has **never** appeared in Part A, but a candidate entering IDC should know it, and it fits the "art/design history" syllabus line. Ranked below the culture cluster.

## Q4. Design principles regularly tested

### Takeaway
Since 2022 every paper has had at least one "applied principle" item: percentile choice, task heights, colour harmony, Gestalt, UI component semantics, user-research stakeholders, materials and processes. These are higher-yield than pure history because the answer can be reasoned out, and they repeat.

### Cited Findings and card rules

| Principle | Rule to teach | Paper evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropometric percentiles | **Clearance** dimensions (seat breadth, door height) → design for **95th** (large users fit); **reach/height-limited** dimensions (washbasin height, shelf reach, controls) → **5th** (small users reach), often 5th *female*; **adjustable or average-tolerant** dimensions (seat height when not adjustable) → **50th** | 2022 Q20 (C: shortest 5% female); 2026 Q39 (B: 50th height, 95th hip breadth) |
| Work heights | Different tasks need different heights (washing lower, rolling/chopping differ) | 2023 Q25 (D) |
| Door/handle ergonomics | Turning direction conventions for the majority (right-handed) | 2020 Q20 (full marks given) |
| Colour schemes | Analogous (adjacent hues), complementary, split-complementary, triadic, double-complementary; Picasso Blue Period = analogous | 2023 Q23 (C) |
| CMYK / print | C0 M100 Y100 K0 = red; screen angles (K 45°); crop/registration marks | 2015 Q20; 2016-2 Q16, Q36 |
| Gestalt | Similarity, continuity, proximity, closure (P1); + figure–ground, common fate, symmetry, Prägnanz | 2026 Q44 (B) — principle list: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principles_of_grouping) |
| UI components | Radio = one of many; checkbox = many/independent; dropdown = one from a long list; toggle = binary state; text field = free input | 2026 Q37 (D) |
| UX research | Stakeholders include delivery staff, restaurant staff, support, users | 2023 Q14 (all) |
| Usability heuristics | Nielsen's 10 (visibility of status, match with the real world, user control, consistency, error prevention, recognition over recall, flexibility, minimalist design, error recovery, help) | P3 (no direct question yet) — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) |
| Typography | Weights (hairline…black), pica/point, serif vs sans, monospace, font anatomy (x-height, ascender, counter) | 2015 Q13, Q46; 2025 monospace NAT; 2024 Part B criterion |
| Materials & processes | Blown-film extrusion (bags), injection (tiffin box), blow (jerry can), roto (water tank), FRP (boat), sheet forming (spoon), shrink fit (cart rim), Tetra Pak layers | 2015 Q15, Q23; 2016-2 Q32; 2020 Q14; 2021 Q29 |

### Inferences
- The percentile rule is the single most reusable card: two of the last five papers used it, and wrong options are built by swapping 5th and 95th.
- Gestalt and UI-component questions were new in 2026. Expect variants: figure–ground, common region, Fitts's-law-style target sizing (2017 already tested matra key layout speed).

### Gaps
- No official syllabus breakdown says how many principle questions to expect. The frequency figures are inferred from papers.
