# CEED / Design-Entrance Prep Tools Landscape, Learning Science, and AI Possibilities

## Existing products: coaching platforms, mock series, free resources (features, pricing, reviews)

### Takeaway
The market is fragmented: coaching institutes (BRDS, PW) sell books, mock papers and batches; aggregators (Careers360, CollegeDekho, Toppersexam, HenceProve, Silica, Toprankers) sell or give away Part A-style MCQ mocks. Mocks are cheap (free to about Rs 1,000). Part B (drawing) feedback is available only through human faculty in paid batches. The only mock that matches the real interface is IIT Bombay's official one.

### Cited Findings
**Exam context (affects what a tool must cover)**
- CEED 2027 exam date is 17 Jan 2027. Part A is a 1-hour computer-based test (CBT) with NAT, MSQ and MCQ questions (8 NAT/32 marks, 10 MSQ/40 marks, 26 MCQ/78 marks). Part B is 2 hours, pen and paper in an answer booklet. Final score weighting: 25% Part A, 75% Part B. — [Careers360 study plan](https://design.careers360.com/articles/how-to-prepare-for-ceed-online-2027-complete-study-plan-and-resources)
  - Glossary: NAT = numerical answer type (type a number, no options). MSQ = multiple-select. MCQ = single-correct.
- Part B structure conflicts across third-party sources. Some say 5 descriptive questions of 20 marks each (100 marks), covering Sketching, Creativity, Form Sensitivity, Visual Sensitivity and Problem Identification. Another Careers360 table lists 4 sections (80 marks). PW states 150 marks in one place. All agree there is no negative marking in Part B. No official IITB page surfaced in search; verify against the CEED brochure. — [Careers360 exam pattern](https://design.careers360.com/articles/ceed-exam-pattern); [Toprankers](https://www.toprankers.com/ceed-exam-pattern); [CollegeDekho](https://www.collegedekho.com/news/ceed-exam-pattern-syllabus-11512/); [PW](https://www.pw.live/design-architecture/exams/ceed-2026-exam-pattern)
- The Careers360 study-plan page lists Part B as "44 questions / 150 marks". This looks like an error, since other sources say 5 questions. — [Careers360](https://design.careers360.com/articles/how-to-prepare-for-ceed-online-2027-complete-study-plan-and-resources)

**Reviews and complaints**
- A CEED 2020 topper (Pramod P. Ranjan) took no formal coaching. He advised a good mentor or small local training over big branded centres. — [Careers360 topper interview](https://design.careers360.com/articles/ceed-2020-topper-pramod-p-ranjan-says-design-mix-of-both-passion-and-ambition)

### Inferences
- Part B carries 75% of the weight but has the thinnest tooling. Part A mocks are a commodity, mostly free or under Rs 1,000. A personal app gains the most from Part B practice and feedback loops, plus a Part A mode that mimics the CBT (timer, question palette, NAT keypad, MSQ partial-marking rules).
- Many aggregator mocks seem to be repackaged MCQ sets with little analytics. Their 2027 labels and small rating counts suggest low quality control.
- The one-hour Part A gate is pass/fail for Part B evaluation. Part A accuracy under time pressure is therefore a hard prerequisite, not a minor section.

## Gaps in existing tools (Part B feedback, analytics, spaced repetition, spatial drills, CBT simulation, progress tracking)

### Takeaway
Sketch feedback exists only as human faculty review inside paid batches (PW, BRDS courier evaluation). No product found offers spaced repetition for design GK, adaptive spatial-reasoning drills, CEED-faithful CBT simulation outside IITB's own mock, or a longitudinal sketch journal.

### Cited Findings
- PW's drawing feedback is "faculty review drawing assignments and personalised feedback". It is human, batch-bound, and has no stated turnaround. — [PW](https://www.pw.live/design-architecture/design/batches)
- BRDS test papers were historically solved on paper and couriered for evaluation (older pages). — [BRDS](https://rathoredesign.com/ceed-study-material-pdf/)
- Dshala offers "instant result analysis" on quizzes, the only analytics claim found. — [Dshala](https://dshala.com/)
- Toprankers claims "in-depth feedback" but does not say Part B is evaluated or that the interface is simulated. — [Toprankers](https://www.toprankers.com/ceed-mock-test-series)
- Silica withholds mock solutions until the admit card is submitted, which delays the feedback loop. — [Silica](https://silica.co.in/?p=4284)
- ShapeVerse sends students to generic Brilliant.org for spatial practice, suggesting no design-specific spatial drill tool exists in its view. — [ShapeVerse](https://shapeverse.in/blog/free-resources-design-exam-prep/)
- The official mock is the only interface-faithful one. — [Careers360](https://design.careers360.com/articles/ceed-mock-test)

### Inferences
- Gap list for a personal app, in order of exam weight:
  1. Fast, rubric-based Part B feedback, matched to the 5 section types.
  2. A sketch journal with before/after comparison over time.
  3. CBT-faithful Part A timing drills.
  4. Spaced-repetition design GK (art movements, designers, Indian crafts, product history).
  5. Adaptive spatial drills (rotation, folding, hidden figures).
  6. An error log that tags mistakes by type.
- None of the products found combine these in one place. Aspirants likely stitch them together from separate tools.

### Gaps
- No user-review data confirms these gaps from the aspirant side; they rest on feature listings.

## Learning science applicable to a personal prep app

### Takeaway
Strong evidence supports retrieval practice and spaced practice, the only two "high utility" techniques. Interleaving helps moderately, and most strongly for visual and art categorisation. Spatial skills, including mental rotation, are trainable with moderate, durable, transferable effects. Drawing improvement via deliberate practice is plausible, but this pass found no sourced effect size for it.

### Cited Findings
- **Dunlosky et al. 2013** reviewed 10 techniques. Only practice testing (retrieval) and distributed (spaced) practice were rated high utility. Summarisation, highlighting and rereading were rated low utility. Caveat: benefits depend on motivated, capable learners. — [APS release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html); [Kent State](https://www.kent.edu/kent/news/success/all-study-strategies-not-created-equal-according-kent-state-researchers)
- **Uttal et al. 2013** meta-analysed 217 spatial-training studies. Average training effect g = 0.47 (SE 0.04), a moderate effect. Effects were durable (unchanged by delay before posttest) and showed transfer. — [PubMed 22663761](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22663761/); [full PDF](https://www.gamesandlearning.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/metaanalysis-Uttal_Newcombe.pdf)
  - Glossary: Hedges' g is a standardised difference between groups. 0.2 is small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large.
  - A secondary source reports g = 0.61 for children under 13. — [PMC7379338](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7379338)
- **Sorby (Michigan Tech):** spatial-visualisation courses for students scoring low on the PSVT:R (Purdue Spatial Visualization Test: Rotations) were linked to higher grades and retention, especially for women. Critics note the designs are mostly quasi-experimental, so self-selection is a confound. — [Sorby & Veurink EDGD](https://edgd.asee.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/22/2019/09/Impact-of-Visualization-Training-on-Student-Leaving_Sorby-Veurink_67th-EDGD-Proceedings.pdf); [ASEE](https://peer.asee.org/27470.pdf); [critique summary](https://escholarship.org/content/qt7p6978b8/qt7p6978b8.pdf?t=op2luf)
- **Brunmair & Richter 2019** interleaving meta-analysis (59 studies, 238 effect sizes): overall g = 0.42. Effects were largest for paintings (g = 0.67) and visual materials. Interleaving is not advised for expository texts and word lists. Heterogeneity is high (I² = 77%). — [PubMed 31556629](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31556629/); [preprint PDF](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf)
- **FSRS vs SM-2 (spaced-repetition schedulers):** Anki's official FAQ says FSRS needs fewer reviews than its default SM-2 algorithm for the same retention. Secondary estimates of the saving range from 15% to 50%, commonly 20–30%. The FSRS optimiser and benchmark are open source, and it works best once a review history exists. — [Anki FAQ](https://faqs.ankiweb.net/what-spaced-repetition-algorithm); [Borretti, FSRS in 100 lines](https://borretti.me/article/implementing-fsrs-in-100-lines)
  - Glossary: FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler) models memory stability per card to choose the next review date.

### Inferences
- **Design GK** (art movements, designer–product pairs, styles): use FSRS flashcards with interleaved image-based cards, e.g. "which movement is this chair?". This matches the paintings result (g = 0.67).
- **Part A:** use mixed (interleaved) timed sets instead of topic blocks, plus retrieval and an error log that re-queues missed question types on a spaced schedule.
- **Spatial drills** (rotation, folding, sections): the evidence supports a short daily dose. Uttal suggests gains persist. Track accuracy and response time to show progress.
- **Drawing:** apply deliberate-practice principles (a specific sub-skill each session, immediate feedback, repetition at the edge of ability) through rubric-tagged critiques and a weekly re-draw of the same prompt for comparison. This is inferred, not sourced here.

### Gaps
- No source retrieved on deliberate practice for drawing (e.g. Ericsson-style studies on observational drawing), or on error-log efficacy.
- No effect sizes for Sorby's courses were retrieved.
- Uttal's adult-only and mental-rotation-only subgroup effects were not extracted.

## AI and tech possibilities (vision-LLM sketch critique, generated spatial puzzles, prompt generators, journals, flashcards)

### Takeaway
Vision-language models (VLMs, AI models that read images and text) can approach expert agreement on rating design sketches for drawing quality, uniqueness and creativity, but only with few-shot calibration examples and reasoning. They are harsher on hand-drawn sketches, and on diagram-correctness tasks they still trail humans. Use them as a rubric-bound coach, not a grader of record.

### Cited Findings
- **Edwards, Tehranchi, Miller & Ahmed** ("AI Judges in Design"; arXiv 2025, J. Mechanical Design 2026). With text-plus-image in-context learning (showing the model rated example sketches), the best reasoning VLM reached expert-level agreement on uniqueness and drawing quality in the preprint. It matched or beat trained novices on all metrics. The journal version is more cautious: it outperforms two-thirds of trained novices and approaches expert-equivalence on uniqueness, creativity and drawing quality, with some cases exceeding expert–expert agreement on specific tests. — [arXiv 2504.00938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00938v1); [MIT DeCoDE PDF](https://decode.mit.edu/assets/papers/2026_Edwards_judges_jmd.pdf)
- **SketchJudge (arXiv 2601.06944, Jan 2026)** is a benchmark of 1,015 hand-drawn student STEM diagrams that tests error diagnosis. Even advanced models "trail humans significantly". — [arXiv 2601.06944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06944v1)
- A study of six multimodal LLMs, zero-shot, rated the creativity of sketches and AI images. Correlation with human ratings on hand-drawn sketches was roughly 0.29–0.68. Models were harsher on hand-drawn sketches. Reasoning chains improved interpretability but not alignment. This came via an aggregator, so verify it. — [emergentmind listing](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2603.28363); see also [par.nsf.gov](https://par.nsf.gov//servlets/purl/10635108)

### Inferences
- **Feasible now:** photo upload, then VLM critique against a fixed CEED Part B rubric (proportion, perspective, line quality, idea clarity, annotation, problem relevance). Calibrate it with 5–10 rated example sketches, ideally rated by a mentor once. That matches the in-context-learning setup that worked in Edwards et al.
- **Limitations:**
  - Unreliable on precise geometric correctness (SketchJudge).
  - Biased harsh on hand-drawn work.
  - Score drift between sessions, so fix the prompt and examples and log scores.
  - Phone photos lose quality from lighting and skew.
  - Cannot see the process (time spent, construction lines erased).
- **Mitigation:** ask for qualitative "top 2 fixes" rather than absolute scores, and track the trend rather than the number.
- **Spatial puzzle generation:** cube nets, folding, mental rotation (Shepard–Metzler-style block figures) and hidden figures can be generated procedurally in code (e.g. three.js), with answers known by construction. That is more reliable than LLM-generated puzzles, where the answer key may be wrong. This is inferred; no source checked LLM spatial-puzzle accuracy.
- **Drawing prompt generator:** an LLM, or a combinatorial list (object × viewpoint × constraint × user scenario), seeded from past CEED Part B themes and the Careers360 "200 sketching ideas". Pair it with a timer matching section time.
- **Flashcards:** FSRS is available as open-source libraries (ts-fsrs, py-fsrs; existence inferred from the open-source FSRS project), which allows embedding instead of exporting to Anki.

### Gaps
- No study found on VLM feedback improving learners' drawing skill over time, as opposed to rating accuracy.
- No commercial AI sketch-critique product targeting Indian design entrance was found.

## General-purpose tools aspirants combine (Anki, Notion, Pinterest, Behance, Procreate)

### Takeaway
No retrieved source documents aspirants' actual tool stacks. The free-resource guides point to official past papers, design publications, Brilliant.org and Reddit, not Anki, Notion or Pinterest.

### Cited Findings
- ShapeVerse's free-resource list names Brilliant.org, Material Design guidelines, isometric dot paper, Dezeen, Core77, Domus and Wallpaper, and r/DesiDesigners. It does not mention Anki, Pinterest or Behance. — [ShapeVerse](https://shapeverse.in/blog/free-resources-design-exam-prep/)
- Anki supports FSRS natively. — [Anki FAQ](https://faqs.ankiweb.net/what-spaced-repetition-algorithm)

### Inferences
- Likely roles (unsourced): Pinterest and Behance for visual reference and mood-boarding; Notion for planners and error logs; Procreate for digital sketch practice (though Part B is pen and paper, so the transfer is imperfect); Anki for GK.
- The value of a custom app is collapsing this stack into one loop: prompt → timed sketch → photo → critique → journal → spaced re-attempt.

### Gaps
- No survey, Reddit thread or app-review evidence on which general tools CEED aspirants actually use, or why. This needs direct community research (r/DesiDesigners, Telegram groups, YouTube comment sections).
