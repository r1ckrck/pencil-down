# CEED Preparation Strategies, Struggles, and Topper Habits

> **Source coverage caveat.** Reddit (r/CEED, r/designindia) is blocked for this researcher's search and fetch tools, and Medium plus Shiksha topper interviews returned HTTP 403. Firsthand accounts below therefore come from blogs (TEAcup, Stuff You Look, DesignEx Studio, DQ Labs) and search snippets. Many firsthand accounts predate 2023. 2023–2026 material is mostly coaching or aggregator guides (Careers360, Toprankers, ShapeVerse, CollegeDekho, DesignAspirants), which are commercial and partly unverified.

## Exam structure (context for all prep questions)

### Takeaway
CEED is one 3-hour sitting. Part A is computer-based objective questions and acts as a screen. Part B is 5 pen-and-paper design/drawing/writing questions and carries 75% of the final score, so rank is driven by Part B.

### Cited Findings
- CEED 2027: 17 Jan 2027, 9:00–12:00. Part A = NAT, MCQ and MSQ questions answered on computer. Topics: visual/spatial ability, environmental and social awareness, analytical/logical reasoning, language, observation, design sensitivity. Part B = 5 questions testing design, drawing and writing, answered in a physical answer book. Final score = 0.25 × Part A + 0.75 × Part B. Part A cut-off announced 4 Feb 2027 — [CEED 2027 official](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/)
- 11 participating institutes: IIT Bombay (IDC), IIT Delhi, IIT Gandhinagar, IIT Guwahati, IIT Hyderabad, IIT Jodhpur, IIT Kanpur, IIT Roorkee, IISc Bangalore (CPDM), IIITDM Jabalpur, IIITDM Kancheepuram — [CEED 2027 official](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/)
- Coaching guides give the split as Part A 1 hour and Part B 2 hours, with Part A as a qualifying cutoff and Part B deciding rank — [Toprankers 30-day plan](https://www.toprankers.com/ceed-30-day-plan). The official page gives only the overall 3-hour window — [CEED official](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/)
- **Conflicting/unreliable:** ShapeVerse (Apr 2026) claims Part A is 180 min with 80 single-correct questions, and that Part B is ~3 open problems of 60–90 min each, or a "full day". This contradicts the official pattern and the guide is internally inconsistent — [ShapeVerse guide](https://shapeverse.in/blog/ceed-2027-complete-preparation-guide/)
- Topper scores are modest: CEED 2025 AIR 1 Rohit Ravindra Waghmare scored 82.38/100. CEED 2023 AIR 1 Khyati Rathore scored 68.85, AIR 2 66.63. CEED 2022 top score 67.725. CEED 2020 AIR 1 69.68 — [Careers360 toppers](https://design.careers360.com/articles/ceed-toppers)
- CEED 2025: ~4,371 appeared, ~1,848 qualified (aggregator figure, unverified) — WebSearch summary citing [PW CEED topper list](https://www.pw.live/design-architecture/exams/ceed-topper-list)

### Inferences
- Top scores of 65–82% mean nobody "aces" Part B. An app should calibrate expectations: a good attempt gets roughly 60–70% of Part B marks.
- Part A is a gate, Part B is the rank. Prep effort and app feature weight should skew toward Part B.

### Gaps
- Official per-part durations, question counts and marking scheme sit in the Information Brochure, which was not fetched.

## 1. Prep timelines and weekly routines

### Takeaway
Typical windows: 30–40 day crash plans, a ~12-week full-time plan (4–6 h/day), and 4–8 months for working professionals (2–3 h/day plus heavier weekends). Strong candidates have started as late as ~4 months out (AIR 4, 2021). All sources agree that daily consistency beats long cram sessions, and that sketching should happen every day.

### Cited Findings
- **CEED 2021 AIR 4 (Apoorv Anurag, B.Tech Civil):** started CEED-only prep at end of September for a mid-January exam (~3.5 months), after a failed attempt to split time with GATE. Drew every day. Took about a dozen online mocks in the final weeks — [TEAcup Blog](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2021/08/apoorv-ixd-at-idc-iitb.html)
- **CEED 2017 rank 15 (Srinag KA):** started ~1 year before. Joined DQ Labs coaching and followed a topic-sequenced timetable — [DQ Edge](https://edge.dqlabs.in/articles/ceed-topper-interview)
- **CEED 2014 AIR 10 (Ashish Verma, B.Tech Mech):** found out about CEED in late July 2013 and prepped minimally alongside GATE. Later chose IISc CPDM over IDC — [DesignEx Studio](https://www.designexstudio.com/post/ashishceed-iit-bombay)
- **30-day plan (Toprankers):** Days 1–7 diagnose (untimed PYQ baseline, error map, basics), Days 8–18 build (advanced topics, timed PYQs, Mock 2), Days 19–26 simulate (full 3-hour mocks, speed-drawing 5 concepts in 45 min), Days 27–30 revise only, then rest. Assumes 4–6 h/day. Daily split about **60% Part B / 40% Part A**. Minimum 4–5 timed PYQs and 4 full mocks. Keep an **error log** and a **sketch bank** of 2–3 concept sketches/day (20–25 by Day 30) — [Toprankers](https://www.toprankers.com/ceed-30-day-plan)
- **12-week plan (ShapeVerse, 2026):** 4–5 h/day full-time. Weeks 1–4 foundations (one spatial topic, one design case study, 10 reasoning Qs, one editorial plus one reading passage daily). Weeks 5–8 timed section practice (5 spatial Qs at 2 min each, 15 reasoning Qs daily, full mock by week 7). Weeks 9–12: 20 mixed Qs daily, 30 min/day on the weakest type, 2–3 full timed papers. Working candidates: 2–3 h/day over 16–20 weeks, "many" over 6–8 months — [ShapeVerse](https://shapeverse.in/blog/ceed-2027-complete-preparation-guide/)
- Working professionals: 2–3 h/day, 16–20 weeks, or 6–8 months — [ShapeVerse](https://shapeverse.in/blog/ceed-2027-complete-preparation-guide/), as summarised in search alongside [DesignAspirants](https://designaspirants.com/blog/ceed-preparation-for-working-professionals/)
- Consistency over volume: daily sketch practice beats cramming 5–6 hours at once (Prasanna Gadkari, IISc M.Des, TEAcup blog author) — [TEAcup: How to begin CEED prep](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2013/09/how-to-begin-ceed-preparation.html) (search snippet)
- 40-day crash plan exists as a well-known older template — [TEAcup: CEED in 40 days](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2011/08/ceed-exam-study-timeline.html) (not fetched)

### Inferences
- The common arc is **diagnose → build → simulate → revise**. An app can map this onto phases driven by exam date and available hours/day.
- Two pieces of state recur in good plans: an **error log** (Part A) and a **sketch bank** (Part B). Both are natural core app objects.
- Toppers started anywhere from ~4 months to ~1 year before the exam. Start date matters less than daily regularity and mock volume.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 topper gave a verifiable hour-by-hour weekly routine. Shiksha topper interviews (403) may contain them.

## 2. Part A preparation (visual/spatial, awareness, logic, English)

### Takeaway
Part A is practised mainly through previous-year papers (PYQs), mocks and topic notes built from PYQs. There are no CEED-specific textbooks. Toppers answer only questions they are sure of, because wrong answers carry negative marks. Spatial ability is seen as the highest-leverage section, and GK means design-, environment- and current-affairs reading.

### Cited Findings
- **PYQ-as-syllabus method (AIR 4, 2021):** treated each previous-year question as a topic and made notes on everything related to it. This also exposed weak areas — [TEAcup](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2021/08/apoorv-ixd-at-idc-iitb.html)
- **Exam tactics (rank 15, 2017):** marked uncertain questions, answered sure ones first, did not guess because of negative marking, and read each question 2–3 times — [DQ Edge](https://edge.dqlabs.in/articles/ceed-topper-interview)
- Another topper (Part A 35.2) answered only questions that were "easy to crack" — search snippet of Shiksha topper interviews ([Shiksha](https://www.shiksha.com/design/industrial-product-design/articles/ceed-topper-shares-tips-to-crack-the-exam-blogId-15039), not fetched due to 403)
- **Section weights (approximate, unofficial):** visualization ~30%, environmental/social ~20%, logic ~20%, language ~15%, design thinking ~15% — [ShapeVerse](https://shapeverse.in/blog/ceed-2027-complete-preparation-guide/)
- **Visualization practice:** orthographic, isometric and perspective; paper-folding nets; rotations, reflections and translations. Use GATE civil drawing questions and 3D tools (Fusion 360, Tinkercad) — [ShapeVerse](https://shapeverse.in/blog/ceed-2027-complete-preparation-guide/)
- **Logic:** series, matrices, Venn diagrams, syllogisms, analogies, data interpretation from CAT/GRE/LSAT workbooks — [ShapeVerse](https://shapeverse.in/blog/ceed-2027-complete-preparation-guide/). R.S. Aggarwal *Verbal & Non-Verbal Reasoning* is the only CEED-specific book recommendation found — [Careers360 Q&A](https://www.careers360.com/question-what-are-the-preparation-tips-of-ceed-2021)
- **English:** reading comprehension, sentence sequencing, 10 vocabulary words/day — [ShapeVerse](https://shapeverse.in/blog/ceed-2027-complete-preparation-guide/)
- **GK/awareness sources:** Manorama current-affairs yearbooks (AIR 10, 2014) — [DesignEx](https://www.designexstudio.com/post/ashishceed-iit-bombay). Design Observer, AIGA Eye on Design, TED Design, The Hindu/Indian Express editorials, and design case studies such as Aravind Eye Care and M-Pesa — [ShapeVerse](https://shapeverse.in/blog/ceed-2027-complete-preparation-guide/). NCERT Class 6–8 environmental science plus sustainability/design current affairs, revised in 15–20 min blocks — [Toprankers](https://www.toprankers.com/ceed-30-day-plan)
- **UCEED papers as extra Part A practice:** syllabi overlap and UCEED questions are "somewhat tougher" — [Stuff You Look](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/p/ceed-mdes.html)
- "No good materials (books), esp. for design study" — blog author Bhanu Chander V — [Stuff You Look](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/p/ceed-mdes.html)
- Mock answer keys are sometimes wrong (AIR 4, 2021) — [TEAcup](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2021/08/apoorv-ixd-at-idc-iitb.html)
- Free official PYQs and sample papers are on ceed.iitb.ac.in — [Careers360](https://design.careers360.com/articles/ceed-preparation-strategy) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- The resource landscape is fragmented: PYQs, scattered blogs, coaching mocks of uneven quality and no canonical book. Curated, verified PYQ banks with explained answers fill a real gap, especially given the reports of wrong mock keys.
- Practice needs **confidence tagging** (sure / unsure / skip) to train the negative-marking discipline toppers describe.
- Spatial reasoning suits interactive practice: rotating 3D models, fold/unfold nets and orthographic-to-isometric drills.

### Gaps
- No source quantified how much time toppers gave each Part A section.
- No reliable 2023–2026 source covered how aspirants study Indian crafts, designers or art movements specifically (beyond generic "read GK").

## 3. Part B preparation (sketching, design problems, creativity, time)

### Takeaway
Toppers sketch daily with a progression: warm-ups and lines → basic shapes and 3D forms → products → human figures → vehicles and scenes. Practice is with real pens and pencils because Part B is on paper. They practise PYQ design problems on a timer with a per-question budget. Examiners reward clear thinking and communication over polished rendering. Feedback comes from peers, communities and coaching, or from no one.

### Cited Findings
- **Daily progression (AIR 4, 2021):** warm-ups → basic shapes → 3D forms → products → figures → vehicles. Traced human figures from newspapers to learn poses and proportions. Did Instagram challenges ("100 products in 100 days", human poses, logo design, Inktober) — [TEAcup](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2021/08/apoorv-ixd-at-idc-iitb.html)
- **Volume drill:** one topper (Part B 64) started with circles, ellipses and curves in ballpoint pen, 10 sheets/day — search snippet of [Shiksha topper interview](https://www.shiksha.com/design/articles/ceed-topper-shares-prep-tips-to-crack-part-a-part-b-interview-round-blogId-15054) (403, unverified)
- **Focus areas (rank 15, 2017):** perspective and proportion, shadow and reflection, detailing, and showing thought process and problem-solving. Learned sketching from YouTube and online courses — [DQ Edge](https://edge.dqlabs.in/articles/ceed-topper-interview)
- **Time budget (AIR 4, 2021):** ~18 min per question, last 30 min reserved for finishing: "Keep the last half hour for finishing up only." — [TEAcup](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2021/08/apoorv-ixd-at-idc-iitb.html)
- **Exam kit (AIR 4, 2021):** 4H pencil for construction, 2B for finishing, small colour-pencil set, two rulers, erasers, fineliners, white gel pen. Don't bring large art sets — [TEAcup](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2021/08/apoorv-ixd-at-idc-iitb.html)
- **Speed over perfection (AIR 10, 2014):** speed was the main constraint, so he sketched freehand without scales or compasses — [DesignEx](https://www.designexstudio.com/post/ashishceed-iit-bombay)
- **Ideas over rendering:** CEED is "more of ideas rather than sketching", and portrait skill alone won't carry you. Show at least two views (3D recommended) and exploded views for mechanisms. Don't prepare for only one design stream, since past papers included UI design and story writing — [Stuff You Look](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/p/ceed-mdes.html)
- Rough sketches with clear reasoning often score better than polished renders (guide claim) — [ShapeVerse](https://shapeverse.in/blog/ceed-2027-complete-preparation-guide/)
- **Drawing technique tips:** draw from shoulder/arm, not wrist, for confident lines; focus early on proportion, shape, size, line quality; timed sketching; review your own sketches critically. Practise traditionally because the exam bans digital media — WebSearch summary of [Careers360 drawing techniques](https://design.careers360.com/articles/drawing-techniques-and-strategies-from-uceed-toppers), [Careers360 traditional vs digital](https://design.careers360.com/articles/traditional-vs-digital-sketching-which-to-focus-on-for-uceed-2026), [CollegeDekho Part B tips](https://www.collegedekho.com/articles/uceed-2026-part-b-sketching-tips-for-beginners) (UCEED-focused but applicable)
- Don't only sketch: design-aptitude/problem questions carry a large share of Part B marks — WebSearch summary of [Shapeverse Part B guide](https://shapeverse.pages.dev/blog/uceed-part-b-drawing-guide/)
- **Structured design-problem practice (2026 guide):** weeks 1–4 one problem/week with research brief; weeks 5–8 generate 10–15 rough ideas in 2 h then develop one; weeks 9–12 timed 75-min sessions (10 brief / 20 research / 30 ideation / 15 final sketch) — [ShapeVerse](https://shapeverse.in/blog/ceed-2027-complete-preparation-guide/) (timings built on their disputed exam model)
- **Speed drills:** 5 concepts in 45 min. Weekly sketch-bank themes: everyday objects → environments/scenes → redesign tasks → expressive human poses — [Toprankers](https://www.toprankers.com/ceed-30-day-plan)
- **Idea-generation habit:** observe your surroundings and question how everyday things are designed; talk to peers for fresh perspectives — [DQ Edge](https://edge.dqlabs.in/articles/ceed-topper-interview); [DesignEx](https://www.designexstudio.com/post/ashishceed-iit-bombay)
- **Feedback sources:** get daily sketches critiqued by fellow aspirants (AIR 4, 2021). A CEED WhatsApp resource group and Instagram/Facebook pages were used — [TEAcup](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2021/08/apoorv-ixd-at-idc-iitb.html). Compare your PYQ solutions with others' work, and on weekends judge progress against your own earlier work — [Stuff You Look](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/p/ceed-mdes.html). Coaching faculty taught one topper how to present ideas — [DQ Edge](https://edge.dqlabs.in/articles/ceed-topper-interview)

### Inferences
- Feedback is the clearest structural gap. Self-study candidates depend on peers, WhatsApp/Instagram groups or paid coaching, with no rubric. A rubric (proportion, perspective, line quality, idea clarity, labelling/views, problem framing) and side-by-side comparison with your own past work are cheap, high-value features.
- Part B practice has two separate tracks, **drawing fluency** (daily volume drills) and **design-problem solving** (timed PYQs). Apps that only do one miss the other.
- Time-boxing per question (~18 min with a 30-min buffer, per a 2021 topper) is concrete enough to build into a timed-practice mode.
- Because the exam is on paper, an app's role is logging, timing, prompts, photo capture and review, not digital drawing.

### Gaps
- No source describes how Part B is actually graded beyond generic criteria. There is no official rubric.
- No 2023–2026 topper account of the Part B routine was accessible (Shiksha 403, Medium 403, Reddit blocked).

## 4. Common pain points

### Takeaway
Recurring struggles: no reliable feedback on drawings, speed under time pressure, splitting attention with GATE or a job, low confidence from a non-design background, poor-quality or misleading coaching, wrong mock answer keys, and fatigue. Engineers are the core audience and their main gap is visual and design communication, not reasoning.

### Cited Findings
- **Split focus:** juggling GATE and CEED "did not work". Low motivation and self-described lack of confidence early on (AIR 4, 2021) — [TEAcup](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2021/08/apoorv-ixd-at-idc-iitb.html)
- **Burnout guidance:** "if stuck, rest and step away rather than exhausting yourself" (AIR 4, 2021) — [TEAcup](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2021/08/apoorv-ixd-at-idc-iitb.html)
- **Speed:** main constraint for AIR 10 (2014). Had no software skills and relied on pencil — [DesignEx](https://www.designexstudio.com/post/ashishceed-iit-bombay)
- **Coaching quality:** centres overstated faculty credentials, and faculty left mid-course or were part-time. Check whether faculty are actually from IIT or NID — [DesignEx](https://www.designexstudio.com/post/ashishceed-iit-bombay)
- **Mock quality:** answer keys are sometimes wrong — [TEAcup](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2021/08/apoorv-ixd-at-idc-iitb.html)
- **Working professionals:** prepare on weekends and keep the job rather than taking a full year off. Coaching is optional — [Stuff You Look](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/p/ceed-mdes.html). 2–3 h/day over 16–20 weeks or 6–8 months — [ShapeVerse](https://shapeverse.in/blog/ceed-2027-complete-preparation-guide/). Use real work experience to add depth to Part B answers — search summary of [DesignAspirants](https://designaspirants.com/blog/ceed-preparation-for-working-professionals/)
- **Background:** engineering grads are eligible and a main audience. No age limit or work-experience cap. Mechanical engineers are described as among the most successful CEED candidates — search summary of [DesignAspirants eligibility](https://designaspirants.com/blog/ceed-exam-eligibility-2027/) (unverified claim). Toppers from civil (2021) and mechanical (2014) backgrounds confirm non-design grads reach top 10 — [TEAcup](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2021/08/apoorv-ixd-at-idc-iitb.html); [DesignEx](https://www.designexstudio.com/post/ashishceed-iit-bombay)
- **Narrow prep:** preparing for a single design stream is "the worst practice" — [Stuff You Look](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/p/ceed-mdes.html)
- **Resource scarcity:** "no good materials (books)" for design study — [Stuff You Look](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/p/ceed-mdes.html)

### Inferences
- The main user archetypes are (a) final-year engineering students, often juggling GATE or placements, and (b) working professionals with 2–3 h on weekdays plus weekends. Both need short daily units and a weekend "long session" mode.
- Confidence and identity ("am I a designer?") come up in accounts. Progress made visible (sketch timeline, before/after) addresses this directly.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data (surveys) on pain-point prevalence was found.
- Reddit, where candid struggle and burnout threads would likely live, was inaccessible.

## 5. Topper habits, mistakes, scores

### Takeaway
Repeated habits: daily sketching, PYQ-driven study, roughly 12+ timed mocks, strict negative-marking discipline, observation journaling, peer critique, and handmade or self-initiated projects. Repeated mistakes: guessing, over-polishing, single-stream prep, and trusting unvetted coaching or mock keys.

### Cited Findings
- AIR 4 2021: ~12 online mocks, daily drawing, PYQ-to-topic notes, 18-min/question budget, peer critique, Instagram challenges — [TEAcup](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2021/08/apoorv-ixd-at-idc-iitb.html)
- Rank 15 2017: one-year prep, coaching, mocks + PYQs, no guessing, perspective/shadow/detailing focus. Built a handmade product he credits for admission — [DQ Edge](https://edge.dqlabs.in/articles/ceed-topper-interview)
- AIR 10 2014: fast freehand sketching, current-affairs books, observing everyday objects — [DesignEx](https://www.designexstudio.com/post/ashishceed-iit-bombay)
- Score benchmarks: AIR 1 82.38 (2025), 68.85 (2023), 67.725 (2022), 69.68 (2020) — [Careers360](https://design.careers360.com/articles/ceed-toppers)
- Interview prep habit: one topper spent 3 months on the portfolio and questioned the background theory of each work. The "why" matters more than the "what/how", and plagiarism is a warning sign — search snippet of [Shiksha topper interviews](https://www.shiksha.com/design/articles/ceed-topper-shares-prep-strategy-blogId-15074) (403, unverified)

### Inferences
- "Mocks plus error review" and "daily sketch plus critique" are the two loops every topper ran. These are the core practice loops for the app.

### Gaps
- No accessible first-person account from 2023–2026 AIR holders. Names are known (Waghmare 2025, Rathore 2023, Gayathri Sajeevan 2026 unofficial AIR 4) but their interviews were not reachable.
- YouTube transcripts were not covered.

## 6. Post-CEED rounds: portfolio, studio test, interview (IITs, IISc)

### Takeaway
CEED itself has no portfolio. Each institute runs its own round (studio/written test, design tasks, portfolio review, interview), usually March–May. IDC IIT Bombay emphasises a hands-on studio exercise plus interview, IISc CPDM tests engineering fundamentals, and some disciplines give take-home tasks. Toppers advise starting the portfolio early because the CEED → interview window is roughly 4–8 weeks.

### Cited Findings
- CEED requires no portfolio. IDC uses a studio exercise for shortlisted candidates plus an interview, where bringing a portfolio is "useful and often expected". Other IITs vary: studio exercise, interview, portfolio review or written exercise. Suggested portfolio: 3–5 projects, each explainable in 2–3 min, showing process (problem → research → ideation → development → outcome). Print ≤30 pages A4/A3. PDF <10 MB, ~20–25 pages. Rounds run Mar–May, ~4–8 weeks after scores — [ShapeVerse portfolio guide, Jun 2026](https://shapeverse.in/blog/ceed-portfolio-preparation-guide/)
- **IDC 2024 (AIR 122 candidate, Medium):** written test at IITB. In 2024 it was a paper prototype of a bench for elderly users in a residential park. The interview covered portfolio, the written test and motivation for Industrial Design — search snippet of [Medium: Navigating CEED MDes Admissions at IDC](https://gore-des.medium.com/navigating-ceed-mdes-admissions-at-idc-iit-bombay-5d50ff8bb2bc) (page 403, details unverified)
- **IDC 2021 Interaction/Communication (AIR 4):** application 1–8 Apr. Three tasks per discipline (poster design, article reviews, critique a bad design). Shortlist 26 Apr based on CEED score plus tasks. Portfolio 8–14 May, interview 18 May. Portfolio had 4 projects (1 UI/UX, 2 graphic, 1 comic character) plus sketches. Questions covered portfolio, tasks, discipline choice, his civil-engineering background, and software (for IxD). Advice: start tasks immediately and build the portfolio early — [TEAcup](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2021/08/apoorv-ixd-at-idc-iitb.html)
- **IISc CPDM vs IDC (2014):** IISc's own test had aptitude plus engineering questions (attempt 5 of 11) and a design part (redraw objects, redesign everyday products). The IISc interview was tough on engineering fundamentals, motivation and communication, and the portfolio drew little interest. IDC was friendlier, the portfolio mattered, and there was a materials test for Product Design. IDC emphasises materials, manufacturing and practicality — [DesignEx](https://www.designexstudio.com/post/ashishceed-iit-bombay) (2014, may be outdated)
- Handmade product shown and demonstrated to examiners was "a key factor" in admission (rank 15, 2017) — [DQ Edge](https://edge.dqlabs.in/articles/ceed-topper-interview)
- Admission/seat allocation via JoSAA-style counselling by CEED rank (guide claim, June tentative) — [ShapeVerse](https://shapeverse.in/blog/ceed-2027-complete-preparation-guide/) (unverified. Most M.Des admissions are institute-run.)

### Inferences
- A prep app shouldn't stop at the exam. A "post-CEED" phase (portfolio builder checklist, mock studio exercises such as paper/material prototyping, interview question bank around "why design / why this discipline / your background") serves a real crunch period.
- The interview format differs by institute and discipline. IISc probes engineering fundamentals, IDC probes making and materials, and IxD/Communication probes software and critique. An institute-specific prep track adds value.

### Gaps
- Weightage of CEED score vs. test/interview per institute was not found in accessible sources.
- 2025–2026 interview/studio-test details beyond the 2024 IDC snippet were not found.
