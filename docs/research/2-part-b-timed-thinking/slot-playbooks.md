# CEED Part B: Timed Thinking Playbooks per Slot

Scope: how to think through each of the 5 Part B slots in ~24 min, plus overall Part B time strategy. Exam pattern is not re-researched; see `../1-exam-and-prep/exam-structure.md`.

**Source quality warning.** Public, citable topper and mentor advice on in-exam Part B thinking is thin. Most coaching pages (Careers360, KollegeApply, CollegeDekho, PW) are generic SEO content with no minute splits. Shiksha topper interviews returned 403 and could not be read. The most concrete minute-level source is one long-running prep blog (Stuff You Look, 2013 and 2021). Official papers are the strongest source: each question prints its own criteria and, in 2026, its own allowed operations. Frameworks below that are not cited are marked as **inference** (synthesised from the official criteria plus general design practice). They are not attributed to toppers.

## 1. How do toppers and mentors break down each slot? Are there frameworks?

### Takeaway
No public topper source gives a full per-slot framework. The usable scaffolding comes from three places. First, the official question text: the criteria, and in 2026 the explicit "cut, add, subtract, deform, repeat" verbs for form. Second, a few mentor and blog heuristics: thumbnails first, line before detail, problems over polish. Third, standard design methods (grid and stroke rules, HMW framing) that map directly onto the printed criteria.

### Cited Findings

**Official brief text is the framework (CEED 2026, verbatim points)**
- Q1 Sketching: "Sketch the whole scene from the point of view of a chair kept nearby." Restrictions: "Use only black/HB-series pencils", "Do not use other colours", "Draw only in the given space". Criteria: Character and Expressions; Proportion and Composition; Quality of Sketching; Environment and Props. — [CEED 2026 QP](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/downloads/CEED_2026_Question_Paper.pdf)
- Q2 Creativity: 4 frames. "Use ANY TWO symbols in each frame; Use each symbol ONLY ONCE". Symbols "cannot be modified, but can be rotated, flipped or scaled proportionately". Extra backgrounds, characters and props are allowed. Title required. Frames labelled 1–4 bottom-left. Criteria: Originality; Graphical Narrative (Story Communication); Humour and Surprise; Clarity and Neatness of Sketches. — [CEED 2026 QP](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/downloads/CEED_2026_Question_Paper.pdf)
- Q3 Visual sensitivity: "20 marks = 4 x 5 marks". Four icons: Robotics, Energy Science, Ocean Science, Food Technology. "consistent visual language", "DO NOT use text", "Only BLACK & WHITE". Criteria: Consistency of visual language; Thematic appropriateness; Effective communication; Composition, Clarity and Neatness. — [CEED 2026 QP](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/downloads/CEED_2026_Question_Paper.pdf)
- Q4 Form sensitivity: "Conceptualise and render a usable single seat furniture that is inspired by a sphere... The sphere may be modified by cutting, adding, subtracting, deforming and repeating. However, the essence of the sphere should be retained." Criteria: Uniqueness of the form; Form-Function relevance and relationship; Detailing; Rendering quality. — [CEED 2026 QP](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/downloads/CEED_2026_Question_Paper.pdf)
- Q5 Problem ID: elderly couple, Srinagar to Chennai by train, then bus and autos. Four "How can we…" statements (2 x 4 = 8 marks). Pick ONE and draw TWO solutions, each with a short title (6 x 2 = 12 marks). Criteria: Variety and uniqueness of the problems; Relevance; Practical feasibility of the design solutions; Clarity in communication. — [CEED 2026 QP](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/downloads/CEED_2026_Question_Paper.pdf)

**Sketching (Slot 1)**
- Plan what to show (view, objects) before drawing. Lay the basic line sketch covering the whole required situation first, then refine. Skip detailing if the line drawing used all the time. — [Stuff You Look, Jan 2021](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/2021/01/time-management-new-strategy-CEED-exam.html)
- Advice from a UCEED 2023 AIR 19 ranker (via Careers360): make tiny thumbnail sketches before the final drawing to fix angle and layout ("saves a lot of time"), and plan foreground, middle ground and background. Add a human figure or familiar object for scale. Practise cubes and cylinders, then objects, from several viewpoints and eye levels. — [Careers360, UCEED toppers' drawing strategies](https://design.careers360.com/articles/drawing-techniques-and-strategies-from-uceed-toppers)
- Same source, mistakes: wrong perspective, wobbly or "hairy" hesitant lines, wrong proportions, and excess shading or detail. — [Careers360](https://design.careers360.com/articles/drawing-techniques-and-strategies-from-uceed-toppers)
- No source found that gives a POV-specific method (camera height first, horizon placement). See Inferences.

**Creativity / storyboard (Slot 2)**
- A storyboard needs a "clear beginning, middle and end". Before drawing, define the message, start with rough thumbnails, and keep it simple with minimal text. Use simple figures for emotion and action, and add speech or thought bubbles and arrows to clarify. The sample 4-frame story runs: quiet start, exchange, sudden action, closing reaction, with a reversal as the payoff. Updated May 2026. — [Careers360, storyboarding for UCEED 2027](https://design.careers360.com/articles/how-to-improve-sketching-and-story-boarding-skills-for-uceed)
- For non-sketch questions, give time to ideation and write keywords or ideas on rough paper so they are not lost. — [Stuff You Look, 2021](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/2021/01/time-management-new-strategy-CEED-exam.html)
- General storyboarding guidance: structure the strip like a joke (setup, build-up, punchline or twist), and make every frame advance the narrative or merge it. — [NTU ADM student blog](https://oss.adm.ntu.edu.sg/a160106/ego-part-1-ideation-narrative-development/), [The Rookies](https://discover.therookies.co/2024/05/05/the-secret-room-a-beginners-guide-to-storyboarding/) (non-CEED)

**Visual sensitivity / icons (Slot 3)**
- Icon-set practice (non-CEED, professional): set a grid, align edges and anchors to it, keep stroke weight and corner treatment uniform, and remember that filled icons read heavier than outline icons, so the set needs balancing. — [Cieden icon grids](https://cieden.com/book/sub-atomic/iconography/icon-grids-and-keylines)
- Optical judgement beats strict measurement. Sometimes breaking the grid gives a more balanced set. — [Streamline HQ](https://blog.streamlinehq.com/grids-and-keyshapes)
- No CEED-specific mentor source found for the icon slot.

**Form sensitivity (Slot 4)**
- The 2026 paper itself names the transformation verbs: cut, add, subtract, deform, repeat, with the constraint to keep the sphere's "essence". This is the official operational framework. — [CEED 2026 QP](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/downloads/CEED_2026_Question_Paper.pdf)
- A practice routine advises drawing the same object in 1-point and 2-point perspective, and annotating with material labels, dimension notes and a short rationale. Dated June 2026. — [Design Aspirants](https://designaspirants.com/blog/ceed-part-b-free-paid-resources/)
- Shading a sphere: identify the core shadow first and work dark to light. Over-modelling kills the glow. — [The Drawing Source](https://www.thedrawingsource.com/shading-a-sphere.html) (general drawing)

**Problem identification (Slot 5)**
- Spend more time on identifying problems than on sketching. Keep solutions "very simple but different". Sketch only the problem area, not the whole object. Plain pencil only, unless colour is asked. Don't write long text when sketches are asked for. "Please don't overdo or over show your artistic intelligence." Dated 2013, based on the CEED 2010 cooker question, so **older advice**. — [Stuff You Look, Sep 2013](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/search/label/cooker%20problem)
- Show the thinking process as 3–4 idea sketches plus 1 final. Annotation is among the most effective Part B tips. Snippet-level only. — [search snippet aggregating CollegeDekho / designaspirants](https://www.collegedekho.com/exam/ceed/how-to-prepare)
- Practice method: sketch three concept directions, choose one, and write a two-line rationale. — [Design Aspirants, 2026](https://designaspirants.com/blog/ceed-part-b-free-paid-resources/)
- Stakeholder thinking (systems engineering, non-CEED): stakeholders are people who benefit from or are harmed by a system, and different stakeholders define the same problem differently. — [SEBoK](https://sebokwiki.org/wiki/Identifying_and_Understanding_Problems_and_Opportunities)

### Inferences: per-slot thinking playbooks (synthesised; ~24 min each)

These playbooks are **inference**. They are built from the official criteria, the cited heuristics above, and standard design method. Use them as drill templates, not as quoted topper advice.

**Slot 1: Sketching, unusual POV (2023 ceiling camera, 2025 fox, 2026 chair)**

| Min | Phase | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Read | Underline: whose POV, who is present, what moment or emotion, and medium limits (HB only). List the 4 criteria at the page margin. |
| 2–5 | Set the camera | Decide eye height first. Chair seat ≈ 45 cm: low horizon, adults loom with worm's-eye convergence. Fox ≈ 40–50 cm in undergrowth: foreground foliage frame. Ceiling: plan view with 3-point downward convergence. Mark the horizon or vanishing points. Do 1–2 thumbnails (≈5 cm) to choose the framing. |
| 5–8 | Block | Light construction lines: room or ground plane, then characters as gesture and box figures placed for overlap and depth (foreground, middle, back). Make the POV obvious: include an in-frame cue such as a chair leg edge, the fox's snout, or a lamp rim. |
| 8–18 | Line | Commit outlines with line weight: heavy near, light far. Spend most time on the faces and body language the brief names (2026: the father's smile, the child and dog's enjoyment) to score "Character and Expressions". Add props that tell the setting ("Environment and Props"): kitchen counter, snacks, bowl. |
| 18–22 | Tone | Minimal shading for depth and ground shadows only. No full rendering. |
| 22–24 | Check | Is the POV unmistakable, are all named characters and actions present, is it within the given space? |

- Freeze breaker: the camera-height decision is the first commitment. Everything else follows.
- Failure modes: drawing from a normal standing eye level (misreading the brief), tiny figures with no readable expression, over-shading, and drawing only part of the "whole scene" asked for.

**Slot 2: Creativity, 4-frame story from given symbols**

| Min | Phase | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Read constraints | Write the rules in shorthand: 2 symbols per frame, each used once, rotate, flip and scale only, title, number frames 1–4. Mis-following a hard rule is the cheapest marks to lose. |
| 3–9 | Divergent read of symbols | For each symbol, list 3+ literal and non-literal readings (e.g. a circle as sun, coin, plate, porthole, eye). Pair readings into candidate "worlds". |
| 9–11 | Pick the story | Choose the pairing with a twist in frame 4 (setup, build, turn, punchline). Allocate symbols to frames on paper. Title last, but draft it now. |
| 11–21 | Draw | Same character design in all frames, simple figures, with clear staging per frame (one action each). Vary shot distance (wide, medium, close) for narrative rhythm. |
| 21–24 | Finish | Title, frame numbers, light hatching for clarity. Check that each symbol is used once and is recognisable. |

- Criteria map: Originality means a non-obvious symbol reading. Humour and Surprise means the frame-4 reversal. Narrative means one character and one goal across frames. Clarity means unmodified symbols that remain readable.
- Failure modes: the first-idea cliché (sun = sun), no ending turn, inconsistent character, symbols distorted or reused, and frames overcrowded with detail.

**Slot 3: Visual sensitivity, icon set, logo or ornamental lettering**

| Min | Phase | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Read | Note the set members, audience (2026: "primarily Indian visitors"), colour limit (B&W), no text, and the box size. |
| 2–8 | Concept per item | For each subject, list 3 metaphors (Ocean Science: wave, submarine, coral, conch) and pick the one that is least generic but still instantly readable. Add an Indian cue where the theme asks for it. |
| 8–11 | Define the system | Before drawing finals, write the rules: container shape (circle or rounded square or none), grid (e.g. 4x4 within each square), single stroke weight or fill-vs-outline choice, corner radius, perspective (flat 2D front view only), and a shared motif. |
| 11–21 | Execute | Draw all 4 in parallel, stage by stage (frames, then main shape, then details), so they stay consistent and none is left unfinished. Use a ruler or compass only if allowed. Keep detail low enough to read at small size. |
| 21–24 | Check | Squint test for equal visual weight. Is any icon heavier or busier? |

- Logo and lettering variant (2023 IWHA, 2024 "Forest" in an ornamental font): extract the given letters' rules (stroke contrast, terminals, ornament motif, x-height), list them, then apply them to each new letter. Consistency is the criterion.
- Failure modes: mixed styles (one outline, one filled, one 3D), over-detailed illustrations instead of icons, text sneaking in, and the last icon rushed.

**Slot 4: Form sensitivity, product or furniture from a primitive or abstract form**

| Min | Phase | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Read | Note the function (single seat, living room), the given form, the allowed operations (cut, add, subtract, deform, repeat), and that the essence must be retained. |
| 2–9 | Operation matrix | 5–8 thumbnails, roughly one per operation. Cut (sphere sliced as a shell seat), subtract (scooped seat cavity), repeat (cluster of balls as a modular seat), deform (squashed sphere as a pouf), add (sphere back on spherical-segment legs). Keep the sphere reading in silhouette. |
| 9–11 | Select | Choose the one that best satisfies both "uniqueness" and "usable": seat height ≈ 40–45 cm, a back or lean surface, stability, and how one sits. |
| 11–20 | Render | One large hero view in 2-point or 3/4 perspective. Single light source and consistent shadow, with a material indication (wood, fabric, metal) via tone and texture. Add a cast shadow to ground it. |
| 20–24 | Detail and annotate | Small side view or detail callouts (joinery, cushion, base), a human silhouette for scale, material labels, and 1-line rationale tying form to function. |

- Showing 2–3 small exploration thumbnails beside the hero rendering may evidence "uniqueness" thinking. This is inference: no source confirms examiners reward visible exploration in this slot.
- Failure modes: a generic armchair with a ball stuck on (essence not used), sphere kept intact so it isn't usable (rolls, no seat), all time spent rendering one weak idea, and inconsistent light direction.

**Slot 5: Problem identification (annotated scene; 2026 HMW + 2 solutions)**

| Min | Phase | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Read | Note the deliverable split and marks (2026: 8 for 4 HMWs, 12 for 2 solutions to one HMW). The time split should follow the marks. |
| 2–8 | Journey and lens sweep | Walk the timeline (home, station, platform, boarding, onboard 2–3 days, transfers, auto, hospital). At each step, apply lenses: user (age, mobility, health, language: Kashmiri or Urdu vs Tamil), activity (carrying luggage, toilets, medicines, food, sleep, payments, wayfinding), and environment (climate shift, crowds, steps, heat), plus other stakeholders (TTE, auto driver, porter, co-passengers). Dump 10–15 raw problems as keywords. |
| 8–11 | Select and phrase | Pick 4 that differ in type (physical, informational, social, emotional or safety) and stage. Write as "How can we [verb] [for whom] [in what context]…". Keep them specific, not "make travel easier". |
| 11–13 | Pick the HMW to solve | Choose the one where two clearly *different* solution directions exist (e.g. product vs service). |
| 13–22 | Two solutions | Each solution in its box: a simple sketch of it in use, with the user and context shown, 2–4 arrow annotations, and a short title. Make the two solutions differ in principle, not just in form. |
| 22–24 | Check | Titles present, statements start with "How can we", and feasibility (uses existing materials, Indian cost context). |

- Older variant (2023 bus stop, 2024 rural kitchen, 2025 veg cart): draw one scene, then mark numbered problem callouts with a one-line "why". Variety across user groups (2025 needed buyer *and* seller) scores better than five versions of one problem.
- Failure modes: problems that are all one type, vague statements, solutions that are tech-magic (an app for everything, robots), over-drawn scenes leaving no time for annotations, and solving more than one HMW when one is asked.

## 2. Typical time splits: per question, in-question phases, thinking vs drawing

### Takeaway
The only concrete CEED-specific split (Stuff You Look, 2021) is this: read all questions first (2–5 min each), lay the sketch's line drawing in about 15–20 min, then do the easiest question next, and return for detailing only with leftover time. The 2-hour / 5-question load gives ≈24 min each. A reasonable inferred in-question ratio is ≈35–45% thinking (read + ideate + select) to ≈55–65% execution. For Slot 5 it is closer to 50/50, per the "more time on identification than sketching" advice.

### Cited Findings
- Read every question at the start for at least 2–5 minutes each, "Mind the evaluation criteria". — [Stuff You Look, Jan 2021](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/2021/01/time-management-new-strategy-CEED-exam.html)
- Sketching question: up to 25–30 min in total, but not all at the start. Complete the key elements (proportion, perspective, size, shape, clarity, line quality) within 15–20 min. — [Stuff You Look, 2021](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/2021/01/time-management-new-strategy-CEED-exam.html)
- Ordering: (1) read all and plan, then line-sketch Q1. (2) Do the easiest remaining question next, outlined quickly from ideation. (3) After all are attempted, complete the four non-sketch answers in detail. (4) Use leftover time for sketch detailing. — [Stuff You Look, 2021](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/2021/01/time-management-new-strategy-CEED-exam.html)
- Tick the on-screen box for each answered Part B question, or it may not be evaluated. — [Stuff You Look, 2021](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/2021/01/time-management-new-strategy-CEED-exam.html); official: candidate ticks on-screen box — [CEED 2027 Brochure](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/downloads/CEED2027_Brochure.pdf)
- Problem ID: an author's first illustration took 10–15 min. "You cannot spend hell lot of time just for detailing" on a 20-mark question. 2013, **older**. — [Stuff You Look, 2013](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/search/label/cooker%20problem)
- "Time management was crucial because 5 questions had to be completed in 2 hours". Some questions demanded visual storytelling plus explanation, which took extra time. 2026 analysis. — [Careers360 CEED 2026 analysis](https://design.careers360.com/articles/ceed-2026-exam-analysis)
- Generic 2026 advice: allocate time per question so all parts are attempted, balancing thinking, writing and sketching. No minute split given. — [KollegeApply, Jan 2026](https://news.kollegeapply.com/news/ceed-2026-part-b-preparation-answer-writing-sketching-tips)
- Analogous (NID Studio): allocate fixed blocks for ideation, construction and finishing. Brainstorm 2–3 ideas quickly, then simplify. "Think first, then make". — [search snippets: Careers360 / PW NID Mains articles](https://www.pw.live/design-architecture/exams/dos-and-donts-for-nid-dat-mains-2025)

### Inferences
- **Recommended Part B clock (inference, 120 min):**

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:06 | Skim all 5 prompts plus criteria. Jot 2–3 keyword seeds for each non-sketch question on rough or margin space. Seeding early lets ideas incubate. |
| 0:06–0:28 | Q1 Sketching: line drawing plus expressions complete (leave tone). |
| 0:28–0:50 | Strongest of Q2–Q5. |
| 0:50–1:12 | Next. |
| 1:12–1:34 | Next. |
| 1:34–1:54 | Weakest (still a full attempt). |
| 1:54–2:00 | Buffer: tone pass on Q1 or fill gaps. Verify on-screen ticks. |

- Hard rule: move on at the time stamp even if unfinished. A complete-but-plain answer beats a half-rendered one, because criteria are spread over 4 dimensions and partial marking exists (no negative marks in Part B — [Brochure](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/downloads/CEED2027_Brochure.pdf)).
- The doing-Q1-first order (Stuff You Look) conflicts with "easiest first" logic. Reasonable compromise: if sketching is a strength, open with it to warm up. Otherwise open with the most confident slot. Booklet pages are question-designated, so order is free.
- Inferred in-question ratio by slot (thinking : making): Sketching 20:80; Creativity 45:55; Icons 40:60; Form 40:60; Problem ID 50:50 (2026 format: 13 min to HMWs, 11 min to two solutions, matching 8:12 marks roughly).

- No source tested question ordering against outcomes.

## 3. What distinguishes high-scoring answers?

### Takeaway
The official answer is the printed criteria: typically 4 per question, read as ≈5 marks each, though the weighting is not officially stated except for Q3 2026 (4 x 5) and Q5 2026 (8 + 12). Sources consistently say clarity and idea beat artistic polish, annotation helps, and over-detailing hurts. No published model answers exist, so "what toppers drew" cannot be verified.

### Cited Findings
- Evaluation is holistic, double-blind, with no model solutions and no re-evaluation. — [CEED 2027 Brochure](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/downloads/CEED2027_Brochure.pdf)
- 2026 criteria explicitly reward "Humour and Surprise" (Creativity), "Uniqueness of the form" (Form), and "Variety and uniqueness of the problems" plus "Practical feasibility" (Problem ID). — [CEED 2026 QP](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/downloads/CEED_2026_Question_Paper.pdf)
- Examiners look for "concept clarity, creativity, drawing quality and logical explanation". Sketches should be "clear, proportionate and relevant to the concept". "Avoid shading unless it adds value." Avoid long, wordy text. — [KollegeApply, Jan 2026](https://news.kollegeapply.com/news/ceed-2026-part-b-preparation-answer-writing-sketching-tips)
- Problem identification and justification matter more than polish. Solutions should be simple but different. Don't over-show artistry. **2013**. — [Stuff You Look, 2013](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/search/label/cooker%20problem)
- Annotated sketches showing design thinking (material labels, dimensions, rationale) are presented as valued by IIT faculty evaluators. Source asserts this; it is not verified. — [Design Aspirants, Jun 2026](https://designaspirants.com/blog/ceed-part-b-free-paid-resources/)
- Mistakes that undermine good ideas: wrong perspective, hesitant or hairy lines, wrong proportions, excess shading. — [Careers360, UCEED toppers](https://design.careers360.com/articles/drawing-techniques-and-strategies-from-uceed-toppers)
- Candidates who practised sketching with product thinking performed better (2026 analysis; claim unsourced within the article). — [Careers360 CEED 2026 analysis](https://design.careers360.com/articles/ceed-2026-exam-analysis)

### Inferences
- **Criteria-led thinking:** copy the 4 criteria into the page corner before starting and check each before moving on. Each is roughly a quarter of the marks, so a zero on one (e.g. no humour, no environment) caps the score at ~15/20 regardless of drawing skill.
- **Originality signal:** the second or third idea, not the first. A 2-minute divergent list per question is the cheapest originality insurance.
- **Communication over rendering:** titles, labels, numbered frames and arrows are cheap and map onto the "communication / clarity" criteria present in 4 of 5 questions.
- **Over-rendering risk** is highest in Slot 4 (rendering is a criterion but only ~1/4) and Slot 1.

### Gaps
- No official marker commentary, examiner reports, or scored sample answers exist publicly.

## 4. Analogous advice: UCEED Part B, NID DAT Mains / Studio, NIFT situation test

### Takeaway
UCEED and NID advice mirrors CEED's: brief interpretation first, 2–3 quick ideas, simplify, fixed time blocks, and clarity over artistry. NID's "AIR" (Interpretation, Reflection, Realisation) is the one named framework found. NIFT situation-test material was not reached in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- NID Mains "AIR" strategy: Interpretation (what the brief really asks), Reflection (how to present an idea answering it in limited time), Realisation (execution). Brainstorm at least 2–3 ideas, then "simplify the approach" for feasibility within time and material limits. Allocate time for ideation, construction and finishing. — [Careers360, NID scenario questions (search snippet)](https://design.careers360.com/articles/how-to-tackle-scenario-questions-for-nid-mains); [PW NID Mains dos and don'ts (search snippet)](https://www.pw.live/design-architecture/exams/dos-and-donts-for-nid-dat-mains-2025)
- NID Mains "evaluates how you think, how you approach a problem, conceptualize an idea, and express it visually and logically" (snippet). — [Careers360 NID studio test prep](https://design.careers360.com/articles/how-to-prepare-for-nid-studio-test)
- UCEED: thumbnails before the final, a human figure for scale, line weight from day one, and avoid excess shading (AIR 19, 2023). — [Careers360](https://design.careers360.com/articles/drawing-techniques-and-strategies-from-uceed-toppers)
- UCEED storyboards: beginning-middle-end, a reversal payoff, stick figures, minimal text, bubbles and arrows. Blank-page fear: "start with light lines and accept imperfection". — [Careers360, May 2026](https://design.careers360.com/articles/how-to-improve-sketching-and-story-boarding-skills-for-uceed)

### Inferences
- The "AIR" frame maps onto the CEED playbook phases: read → ideate → select → execute. It is a useful mnemonic for an app's timer phases.
- The NIFT situation test is a 3D model test with different materials, so its timing advice transfers weakly. It was not researched further.
