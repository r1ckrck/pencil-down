# Make her commit before minute ten

**The answer:** the Part B tool should be a **phase clock with a forced commit point**, not a sketch coach. Every drill runs the same loop: **Read → Diverge (generate many ideas fast) → Commit (pick one against 3 yes/no checks, within 60 seconds) → Make (she draws on paper while the clock runs) → Debrief (90 seconds, typed)**. Her known weakness, trouble deciding and moving on, is the main design target. The research supports this focus. Creative thinking is trainable, with effect sizes around **d ≈ 0.5–0.75** across three meta-analyses (d and g are standardised effect sizes: 0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large). **Selection** (picking an idea) trains more readily than generation. People are **poor judges of their own best idea** and drift toward safe ones. They also **stop generating too early**, because they wrongly expect ideas to run out. A fixed generation window followed by a forced pick against explicit criteria addresses both failures at once. In each ~24-minute question she should spend about **20–50% of the time thinking**, depending on the slot, and **commit by minute 5 (Sketching), 9–11 (Creativity, Icons, Form) or 11–13 (Problem ID)**. Across the 2 hours, she should move on at **hard time stamps**, because partial marking and four-criterion rubrics reward a complete plain answer over a half-polished one. AI stays **off from reading to commit**. Experiments show that early LLM help (LLM = large language model, e.g. ChatGPT) shrinks idea diversity and ownership. In a ranked exam, distinctiveness is the point. Most per-slot minute splits below are inference: no topper has published a per-slot framework for the current format.

**Evidence tags used throughout:** **[E]** research evidence (peer-reviewed or meta-analytic). **[O]** official CEED paper or brochure. **[P]** practitioner, mentor or topper advice. **[I]** inference, synthesised by this report.

## The tool's job is to force the decision, not judge the drawing

Part B gives **5 compulsory questions × 20 marks in 2 hours**, answered on paper. Each question prints about **4 evaluation criteria**. Marking is partial and holistic, with no negative marks and no published model answers [O] ([CEED 2027 Brochure](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/downloads/CEED2027_Brochure.pdf), [CEED 2026 paper](https://www.ceed.iitb.ac.in/2027/downloads/CEED_2026_Question_Paper.pdf)). Two consequences follow.

- **The thinking is scoreable before any line is drawn.** Each slot rewards something decided in the first third of the time: the camera position (Sketching), the twist (Creativity), the visual system (Icons), the transformation (Form), and the problem framing (Problem ID).
- **Leaving a criterion empty is the expensive error.** If each criterion is worth about 5 marks, missing one (no humour, no environment, no annotation) caps a question near 15/20, whatever the drawing quality [I]. The official per-criterion weighting is printed only for 2026 Q3 (4 × 5) and Q5 (8 + 12) [O].

So the tool owns four things: **the brief, the idea count, the commit, and the clock.** The drawing stays on paper and is never scored.

| Loop phase | What she does | What the tool does | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Read | Underlines constraints and copies the 4 criteria into the page margin | Shows the prompt and criteria; prompts "list the hard rules" | [O] criteria printed; [P] "mind the evaluation criteria" ([Stuff You Look](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/2021/01/time-management-new-strategy-CEED-exam.html)) |
| Diverge | Thumbnails or keywords on rough paper; taps +1 per idea | Counts ideas against a slot target; hides all examples | [E] fixation, creative cliff |
| Commit | Picks one idea, passes the 3-check gate, types a one-line rationale | 60-second hard limit; locks the choice | [E] selection weakness; [I] mechanics |
| Make | Draws on paper | Shows the phase clock and a "move on" cue | [E] controllable time pressure |
| Debrief | Answers 3–4 typed questions after the clock stops | Logs metrics and trends | [E] felt ≠ actual creativity; [I] format |

## Five research findings set the rules for every mechanic

| Finding | Evidence | Strength | What the tool does about it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ideation is trainable, and selection trains best** | Scott, Leritz & Mumford (2004), 70 studies: overall d ≈ 0.68; divergent-thinking outcomes ≈ .75 but real-world performance ≈ .35 ([UVic summary](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/bitstreams/b37e9fbc-05d8-4b41-b192-4969288bacc1/download)). Haase, Hanel & Gronau (2023), 332 effects: g = 0.53. **Figural (drawing-based) methods** and **convergent** training (selecting and refining) worked better than divergent-only training. Multi-component courses scored g ≈ 0.66 ([Bath](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/creativity-enhancement-methods-for-adults-a-meta-analysis/)) | Strong on trainability; abstract-level on subgroups | Drill on the real exam templates, not generic "alternate uses" tasks, because transfer shrinks. Build **commit drills** alongside generation drills. Use a curriculum, not one trick |
| **First ideas and seen examples anchor everything after (fixation)** | Jansson & Smith (1991): seeing an example cut idea count and categories, and its features got copied, even by professionals ([Crilly 2019](https://core.ac.uk/download/158355214.pdf)). One education study links more ideas to less fixation ([Design Society](https://www.designsociety.org/publication/34578/overcoming_design_fixation_through_education_and_creativity_methods)). Defixation instructions help inconsistently ([Linsey et al.](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/JMD041003.pdf)) | Robust effect; remedies mixed | **No examples before an attempt.** Set an idea-count target. Prompt mid-diverge: "next idea shares nothing with idea 1" |
| **Time pressure hurts through evaluation and loss of control, not the clock itself** | Byron et al. (2010), 76 experiments: low-evaluation contexts *raised* creativity; high evaluation lowered it; uncontrollability hurt linearly ([abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/371445)). Amabile (2002), 9,000+ diary entries: pressure usually hurt, except when people focused undisturbed on one task ("on a mission"). People *felt* more creative under pressure than they were ([APA Monitor](https://www.apa.org/monitor/nov02/tickingclock)) | Strong, but from workplace and lab settings, not exams | **Visible, predictable phase timers** (controllability). **No judging during diverge.** Log objective metrics, not self-ratings |
| **People pick their own ideas badly and quit too early** | Rietzschel, Nijstad & Stroebe (2010): people chose feasible, safe ideas over original ones. Telling them to "pick the most original" raised originality but lowered satisfaction ([abstract](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/912990346)). Berg (2016): creators judge *others'* novel ideas better than their own ([Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/balancing-creative-high-wire-forecasting-success-novel-ideas)). Lucas & Nordgren (2020), 8 studies: idea quality held steady or rose over a session, yet people predicted decline and stopped. Explaining the effect reduced it ([Kellogg](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2020/the-creative-cliff-illusion/?p=1)) | Solid, multi-study | **Fixed diverge window** (she can't quit early). **Explicit commit criteria including "not my first idea"** (overrides the safety bias). **Satisficing**: the first idea that passes all checks wins, rather than hunting for the best. A one-time "creative cliff" explainer |
| **Problem finding predicts originality, but its trainability is unproven** | Getzels & Csikszentmihalyi: art students who spent longer exploring and reformulating the problem made work rated more original, and were more successful years later ([MIT Press Reader](https://thereader.mitpress.mit.edu/the-1960s-art-school-experiment-that-redefined-creativity/)). Meta-analysis: problem finding ↔ creativity r = .22 across 40 studies ([UGA](https://openscholar.uga.edu/record/21269?ln=en)) | Correlational; no training study found | **Budget** problem finding (Slot 5: ~6–9 min) rather than skip it. The lens sweep scaffold is [I] |

Design Heuristics are the one structured ideation method with direct evidence for novice designers. They are 77 cards of transformations distilled from expert designers, e.g. "nest", "hollow out", "fold". In two studies, 12-card subsets gave more developed and more creative concepts for engineering and industrial-design students, though uptake was uneven ([Daly et al., IJEE 2012](https://www.ijee.ie/articles/Vol28-2/29_ijee2559ns.pdf), [Kramer et al. 2015](https://advances.asee.org/wp-content/uploads/vol04/issue04/Papers/AEE-16-Kramer.pdf)) [E]. Crazy 8s, SCAMPER and brainwriting have **no peer-reviewed effect sizes** in this search. They are plausible formats, not proven ones.

**The tension to resolve:** Getzels rewards *more* time on the problem, while her weakness is *not leaving* it. The tool resolves this by making exploration time **fixed and protected**: it can't be cut short or extended. Exploration happens, then ends on the clock [I].

## Each slot gets its own 24-minute script with one commit decision

All five scripts below are **[I]**. They are built from the official criteria [O], the few cited mentor heuristics [P], and standard design method. Use them as drill templates, not as quoted topper advice. In the full 2-hour mode, reading happens up front, so each block compresses to about 22 minutes.

### Summary

| Slot | Thinking : making | Commit by | The one decision | Idea target in diverge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Sketching | 20 : 80 | min 5 | Camera height and framing | 2 thumbnails |
| 2 Creativity | 45 : 55 | min 11 | Which symbol readings make a story with a frame-4 twist | 3+ readings per symbol |
| 3 Visual sensitivity | 40 : 60 | min 11 | The written visual-system rules | 3 metaphors per item |
| 4 Form sensitivity | 40 : 60 | min 11 | Which transformation of the primitive | 5–8 thumbnails (≈1 per operation) |
| 5 Problem ID | 50 : 50 | min 11 (HMWs), min 13 (which to solve) | 4 problems of different types, then the one with 2 distinct solution directions | 10–15 raw problems |

**Universal commit gate** (3 yes/no checks, first idea to pass wins) [I, built on Rietzschel E]:
1. **Obeys every hard rule?** (symbols used once, B&W, no text, the POV named in the brief)
2. **Not my first, obvious reading?**
3. **Drawable clearly in the time left?**

### Slot 1 Sketching: commit the camera at minute 5

Template [O]: a scene from an unusual point of view (POV), e.g. a ceiling camera (2023), a fox (2025), a chair (2026). 2024 was a 4-frame action sequence instead. Criteria 2026: character and expressions; proportion and composition; quality of sketching; environment and props.

| Min | Phase | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Read | Whose POV, who is present, what moment or emotion, medium limits (HB only) |
| 2–5 | **Commit camera** | Fix eye height first: a chair seat ≈ 45 cm gives a low horizon, with adults looming. Do 1–2 small thumbnails, then pick |
| 5–8 | Block | Ground plane, then gesture figures in foreground, middle and back. Put a POV cue in frame (chair leg, fox snout, lamp rim) |
| 8–18 | Line | Line weight heavy near, light far. **Most time on the faces and body language named in the brief.** Add setting props |
| 18–22 | Tone | Depth and ground shadows only |
| 22–24 | Check | POV unmistakable? Every named character and action present? |

- [P] Thumbnails before the final, foreground/middle/background planning, and avoiding hesitant lines and excess shading come from a UCEED 2023 AIR 19 ranker ([Careers360](https://design.careers360.com/articles/drawing-techniques-and-strategies-from-uceed-toppers)). "Lay the whole line sketch first, skip detailing if time runs out" comes from [Stuff You Look](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/2021/01/time-management-new-strategy-CEED-exam.html).
- **4-frame variant (2024 type):** the commit is a beat sheet: anticipation → effort → turn → reaction, decided by minute 5.
- **Failure modes:** drawing from a standing eye level (misreading the brief), tiny figures with unreadable faces, over-shading.

### Slot 2 Creativity: generate readings, then commit to the twist

Template [O]: a 4-frame story or interpretation from given symbols or elements. In 2026: two symbols per frame, each used once, rotate, flip or scale only, with a title. Criteria: originality; graphical narrative; **humour and surprise**; clarity.

| Min | Phase | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Rules | Write the hard rules in shorthand. Breaking one is the cheapest way to lose marks |
| 3–9 | **Diverge** | 3+ literal and non-literal readings per symbol (circle → sun, coin, plate, porthole, eye). Pair readings into candidate "worlds" |
| 9–11 | **Commit** | Pick the pairing with a frame-4 reversal (setup, build, turn, punchline). Assign symbols to frames. Draft the title |
| 11–21 | Draw | One consistent character, one action per frame, varied shot distance |
| 21–24 | Finish | Title, frame numbers. Each symbol used once and still recognisable? |

- [P] Beginning–middle–end, thumbnails first, minimal text, and a reversal payoff come from [Careers360, May 2026](https://design.careers360.com/articles/how-to-improve-sketching-and-story-boarding-skills-for-uceed). Writing keywords on rough paper so ideas aren't lost comes from [Stuff You Look](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/2021/01/time-management-new-strategy-CEED-exam.html).
- **Failure modes:** the first-reading cliché (sun = sun), no ending turn, distorted or reused symbols.

### Slot 3 Visual sensitivity: commit to written system rules before drawing anything

Template [O]: a set with a consistent visual language. Examples: an "IWHA" identity (2023), extending an ornamental font (2024), zoo-animal icons (2025), museum-section icons with no text, B&W only (2026). Criteria 2026: consistency; thematic fit; communication; composition and clarity.

| Min | Phase | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Read | Set members, audience, colour limit, text ban, box size |
| 2–8 | **Diverge** | 3 metaphors per subject (Ocean Science → wave, submarine, coral, conch). Choose the least generic one that still reads instantly |
| 8–11 | **Commit the system** | Write the rules: container shape, grid, stroke weight or fill-vs-outline, corner radius, flat front view, one shared motif |
| 11–21 | Execute in parallel | All 4 icons stage by stage (container → main shape → detail), so none is left unfinished |
| 21–24 | Check | Squint test: equal visual weight across the set |

- [P] Grid, uniform stroke, and filled-vs-outline weight balance come from professional icon practice, not CEED sources ([Cieden](https://cieden.com/book/sub-atomic/iconography/icon-grids-and-keylines)).
- **Lettering or logo variant:** list the given letters' rules (stroke contrast, terminals, ornament) and then apply them. Consistency *is* the criterion.
- **Failure modes:** mixed styles, illustrations instead of icons, text creeping in, the last icon rushed.

### Slot 4 Form sensitivity: run the operation matrix, then commit to one

Template [O]: furniture or a product derived from a primitive (sphere 2026, abstract 3D form 2023–2024). 2026 printed the allowed operations: **"cutting, adding, subtracting, deforming and repeating"**, keeping the sphere's essence. Criteria: uniqueness; form–function; detailing; rendering.

| Min | Phase | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Read | Function, given form, allowed operations, "essence retained" |
| 2–9 | **Diverge: operation matrix** | 5–8 thumbnails, roughly one per operation (cut → shell seat; subtract → scooped cavity; repeat → ball cluster; deform → squashed pouf; add → sphere on segment legs) |
| 9–11 | **Commit** | Gate plus a usability check: seat ≈ 40–45 cm high, back or lean surface, stable, clear how one sits |
| 11–20 | Render | One large 3/4 hero view, one light source, material by tone, cast shadow |
| 20–24 | Annotate | Side view or detail callout, human silhouette for scale, material labels, 1-line form→function rationale |

- The printed operation verbs **are** a built-in Design Heuristics deck [O + E]. The tool can show one verb per thumbnail slot.
- **Failure modes:** a generic chair with a ball stuck on, an intact sphere that rolls, all the time spent rendering one weak idea.

### Slot 5 Problem identification: budget problem finding, then commit twice

Template [O]: problems in an Indian everyday setting. A bus stop (2023) and a rural kitchen (2024) were "draw + annotate 5". A vegetable cart (2025) needed both buyer *and* seller problems. 2026 asked for an elderly couple's Srinagar→Chennai trip: **4 "How can we…" statements (8 marks) + 2 drawn solutions to one (12 marks)**. Criteria: variety and uniqueness; relevance; feasibility; clarity.

| Min | Phase | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Read | Deliverable split and marks. **The time split follows the marks** |
| 2–8 | **Diverge: journey × lens sweep** | Walk the timeline (home → station → onboard → transfers → arrival). At each step apply lenses: user (age, mobility, language), activity (luggage, toilets, medicines, payment), environment (climate, crowds, steps), other stakeholders. Dump 10–15 keywords |
| 8–11 | **Commit 1** | Pick 4 that differ in *type* (physical, informational, social, safety) and *stage*. Phrase each as "How can we [verb] [for whom] [in what context]" |
| 11–13 | **Commit 2** | Solve the HMW that has 2 clearly *different* directions (e.g. product vs service) |
| 13–22 | Two solutions | Sketch each in use, with user and context, 2–4 arrow notes and a title. They must differ in principle, not just in form |
| 22–24 | Check | Titles present? All statements start with "How can we"? Feasible at Indian cost levels? |

- HMW ("How might we") is IDEO's framing device. A third-party scoping test says a good HMW yields **≥10 different answers**: 1–2 means too narrow, no starting point means too broad ([WA gov CX](https://your.wa.gov/cx-resources/how-might-we-statements/)) [P]. The tool can run this test with no drawing at all.
- [P, 2013] "Spend more time identifying problems than sketching; solutions very simple but different; don't over-show artistry" ([Stuff You Look](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/search/label/cooker%20problem)). This is older advice, but it matches the 2026 criteria.
- **Failure modes:** five versions of one problem type, vague statements ("make travel easier"), tech-magic solutions (an app or robot for everything), an over-drawn scene that leaves no annotation time.

## The 2-hour clock runs on hard stamps, not on feel

There is no verified topper minute log for the current format. Two cited strategies exist:

- **[P] Read-first, sketch-first:** read all questions first (2–5 min each), line-sketch Q1 in 15–20 min, do the easiest question next, and detail only with leftover time ([Stuff You Look 2021](https://stuffyoulook.blogspot.com/2021/01/time-management-new-strategy-CEED-exam.html)).
- **[P] First pass plus reserve:** the 2021 AIR 4 budgeted **~18 min per question and kept the last 30 min for finishing** ([TEAcup](https://teacupblog.blogspot.com/2021/08/apoorv-ixd-at-idc-iitb.html)).

**Recommended default [I]:**

| Clock | Action |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:06 | Read all 5 prompts and criteria. Jot 2–3 keyword seeds for Q2–Q5 in the margins |
| 0:06–0:28 | Q1 Sketching: line work and expressions complete; leave tone |
| 0:28–0:50 | Strongest of Q2–Q5 |
| 0:50–1:12 | Next |
| 1:12–1:34 | Next |
| 1:34–1:54 | Weakest (still a full attempt) |
| 1:54–2:00 | Tone pass on Q1 or fill gaps. **Tick every on-screen answered box** [O] |

- **Why seed early:** incubation means a break from a problem before returning to it. It helps divergent tasks most, works better after solid preparation, and works best when the break is filled with low-demand work ([Sio & Ormerod 2009](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/56211)) [E]. Margin seeds at 0:00, followed by an hour of other questions, approximate this. That the seeds survive and help in exam conditions is [I].
- **Ordering:** booklet pages are assigned per question, so order is free [O]. Open with Q1 if sketching is a strength, otherwise with the most confident slot [I].
- **Hard rule:** at each stamp, she stops mid-line and moves on. Partial marking plus four criteria makes a complete plain answer worth more than a half-rendered one [O + I].

**Trade-off: hard stamps vs the 30-minute reserve.** The reserve lowers the cost of leaving a question unfinished, but it creates a shared pool that an indecisive person tends to sink into one question. **Pick hard stamps as the default for her**, because they directly train the skip habit. Try the TEAcup reserve in 1–2 mocks and keep whichever leaves fewer empty criteria.

## Drill formats and mechanics worth adopting

### Drills

| Drill | Mechanic | Maps to | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crazy 8s** | Fold paper into 8 panels, ~1 min per idea, 8 min total ([Deep Impact](https://www.deep-impact.ch/en/insights/crazy-8s-design-sprint)) | Form (operation matrix), Icons (metaphors) | [P]; no effect-size study |
| **30 Circles** | 3 min to turn 30 circles into objects. Debrief on quantity, *diversity*, rule-breaking and assumed rules ([IDEO](https://www.ideo.com/journal/build-your-creative-confidence-30-circles-exercise)) | Creativity (symbol readings), Form | [P]; trains fluency vs flexibility |
| **Thumbnail rounds** | Time-boxed, no editing, then pick top 3 ([LUMA](https://www.luma-institute.com/thumbnail-sketching/)) | Sketching (camera), all slots | [P] |
| **HMW scoping test** | Does the statement yield ≥10 answers? | Problem ID | [P] |
| **Design Heuristics / operation cards** | One transformation card per thumbnail slot | Form | [E] small classroom studies |
| **Commit-only drill** [I] | Shown 6 pre-typed ideas (hers, from past sessions), she picks one in 30 s against the gate | Every slot, and directly targets her weakness | Built on Haase (convergent trains well) and Berg (judges cold ideas better) [E] |
| **Class-mode progression** | Short forced rounds that lengthen over a session, as in Line of Action and Quickposes ([LoA forum](https://www.line-of-action.com/forums/topic/benefits-of-standard-mode-vs-class-mode)) | Applied to *thinking* time: 3-min diverge-and-commit rounds → full 24-min blocks | [P] for drawing; [I] for thinking |

### Tool mechanics

| Mechanic | Spec | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Phased timer** | Slot-specific phases (tables above), with an audible cue at each phase change. Always visible and never surprising | Controllability [E]; splits [I]; [P] "fixed blocks for ideation, construction, finishing" (NID advice, [PW](https://www.pw.live/design-architecture/exams/dos-and-donts-for-nid-dat-mains-2025)) |
| **Forced commit** | Diverge ends on the clock. 60-second commit screen with the 3-check gate and a one-line rationale. **If time expires, the most recently starred idea is locked, and she draws it** | Selection bias and creative cliff [E]; auto-lock [I] |
| **Idea counter** | One tap per idea during diverge (no typing mid-flow). Slot target shown. Count does not end the phase early | More ideas ↔ less fixation [E, correlational] |
| **Distinctness tags** | After the clock: one word per idea ("fold", "stack", "split"). Reports **distinct** tags, not total ideas | 30 Circles flexibility logic [P]; metric [I] |
| **Prompt mutation** | Two-slot combinator per template: [primitive] × [function/user], [POV] × [scene], [icon theme] × [audience], [setting] × [user group]. Seeded from 2023–2026 papers | Sharpen.design's two-element model ([UIUX Showcase](https://uiuxshowcase.com/resources/sharpen-design-challenge-generator/)) [P]; templates [O] |
| **Criteria checklist** | Printed criteria pinned during Make. At 2 min left: "every criterion visible on the page?" | [O] |
| **No examples before an attempt** | References or past-answer images appear only in debrief, if at all | Fixation [E] |
| **Debrief (≤90 s)** | Fixed questions: What did I almost pick, and why didn't I? When did I commit vs the cue? Which phase overran? Which criterion is weakest on the page? | Reflection benefit is [I]; objective logging over self-rating is [E] (Amabile) |
| **Metrics** | Per slot: ideas, distinct tags, **seconds-to-commit**, phase overruns, auto-locks triggered. Trend lines | [I]; seconds-to-commit is the direct measure of her weakness |
| **Spaced re-attempt** | Re-serve the same prompt 1–2 weeks later; compare tag sets. Growth shows as new categories | [I]; no ideation-specific study found |

## AI belongs before and after the attempt, never during

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| Meincke, Nave & Terwiesch, *Nature Human Behaviour* 2025 | ChatGPT-assisted brainstorming produced **narrower idea sets in 37 of 45 comparisons**. In one task, **94% of AI-assisted ideas overlapped** ([Wharton](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/2025/new-in-nature-chatgpt-decreases-idea-diversity-in-brainstorming/)) |
| Doshi & Hauser, *Science Advances* 2024 | AI ideas improved stories mainly for less creative writers, but similarity between stories rose **10.7%** ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532)) |
| Anderson, Shah & Kreminski 2024 | More ideas, less distinct across people, and **lower felt ownership** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01536)) |
| Qin et al., CHI 2025, "Timing Matters" | Using an LLM **from the start** rather than after independent ideation cut original ideas and creative self-efficacy (her confidence in her own creativity) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06197)) |
| Frontiers 2025 | LLMs fixate too: most of GPT-4o's ideas fell into conventional categories ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12369561)) |
| Drosos, Sarkar et al. 2025 | AI "provocations" (actionable challenges rather than answers) nudged critical thinking; many effects were non-significant ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17247v1)) |

CEED is a ranked exam judged on "originality" and "uniqueness" [O]. An AI idea she adopts is one that thousands of other aspirants could also be given. **Higher per-idea quality does not offset lost distinctiveness** [E + I].

| Role | When | Allowed? | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prompt generator | Before the attempt | **Yes, later** | Prompt with "generate many, pick the most distinct", which brought GPT-4 closest to human-group diversity ([Meincke, Mollick & Terwiesch](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4708466)) [E]. Log past prompts to avoid repeats. A hand-built combinator is safer and enough for v1 |
| Idea source | Read / Diverge / Commit | **No** | Homogenisation and lost ownership [E] |
| Devil's advocate on her commit rationale | Debrief only | **Yes, later** | 2–3 actionable challenges ("why is this POV unusual?"), never replacement ideas. Kept out of the commit phase because it would lengthen the exact step she struggles with [I] |
| Cliché flag | Debrief, on typed tags | **Yes, later** | "Most people's first thought": framed as information, not a score [I] |
| Drawing judge | — | **No (by decision)** | Out of scope |

**Limits:** the AI sees only typed notes, so its debrief reflects what she wrote, not what she drew. A cliché list shown *before* ideation would itself cause fixation, so it appears only after [I].

## Feature set: build the commit loop first

### Must-have (v1)

| # | Feature | Why it earns v1 | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Prompt bank, 5 slots**: the 2023–2026 papers plus hand-written variants, with the printed criteria and hard rules shown | Templates are stable year to year; criteria are the only official rubric | [O] |
| 2 | **Slot-specific phase timer** with cues, from the five scripts | Makes the thinking:making ratio concrete; predictable clock = controllable pressure | [E] controllability; splits [I] |
| 3 | **Forced commit screen**: 60 s, 3-check gate, one-line rationale, auto-lock on expiry | The core intervention for her decide-and-move-on weakness | [E] selection/cliff; mechanic [I] |
| 4 | **Tap idea counter + post-clock distinctness tags** | Protects generation, measures flexibility, costs no thinking time | [E] fixation (correlational); [P] 30 Circles |
| 5 | **Full 2-hour mode**: 6-min seed phase, hard stamps, forced move-on, final "tick the boxes" reminder | Trains the skip habit across questions, not just within one | [P] + [O]; schedule [I] |
| 6 | **90-second debrief + metrics** (seconds-to-commit, auto-locks, overruns, ideas, distinct tags) with trends | Objective progress; felt creativity is unreliable | [E] Amabile; format [I] |

### Later

### Trade-offs to accept knowingly

| Decision | Recommended | Cost accepted |
|---|---|---|
| Auto-lock on commit timeout vs letting her extend | **Auto-lock** | She will sometimes draw a weaker idea. That is the point: practice values moving on over the perfect pick |
| Tap counter vs typed ideas during diverge | **Tap now, tag after the clock** | Tags written from memory are less precise; thinking time stays untouched |
| Fixed exam splits vs adaptive splits | **Fixed per slot for v1** | Ignores her personal strengths; adaptive tuning needs weeks of metrics first |
| Hard stamps vs a finishing reserve | **Hard stamps** | Less polish on her best question |
| AI in the loop vs none in v1 | **None in v1** | Less novelty in prompts and no automated critique; protects originality and ownership, and saves build time |
| Visible examples vs none | **None before an attempt** | No visual "what good looks like"; none officially exists anyway [O] |

**Confidence note:** the meta-analyses and LLM studies are well sourced. Several were read at abstract or secondary-summary level, including subgroup effects in Haase and Byron's time-pressure detail. The per-slot minute splits, gate questions, auto-lock and metrics are all design inference. They are untested against CEED outcomes, so the tool's own seconds-to-commit and empty-criterion trends are the real test.

## Conclusion

The practical insight is that her stated weakness and the research's strongest selection findings point to the same mechanic. People quit generating too early and then pick the safe idea. A fixed diverge window followed by a timed, criteria-gated pick corrects both, and it is exactly the skill Part B rewards in its first eight to ten minutes. This turns the tool from a timer into a **decision trainer**, with **seconds-to-commit** as its headline metric rather than drawing quality, which it deliberately never sees.

The second implication is restraint about AI. In a ranked exam judged on originality, the documented homogenising effect of LLM ideation is a direct cost. AI earns a place only at the edges: generating prompts and challenging her rationale after the attempt. Everything between reading the brief and the first committed line should be hers alone.
