# Drills and Tools for Timed Design Thinking (CEED Part B prep)

Exam context: CEED Part B has 5 compulsory questions (sketching, creativity, form sensitivity, visual sensitivity, problem identification), 20 marks each, 100 total. It is pen and paper, 2 hours, with holistic marking against predefined criteria per question type and no negative marking. Past papers include character design, a 2D icon, and problem identification on a toothbrush and a mixer grinder — [Careers360 exam pattern](https://design.careers360.com/articles/ceed-exam-pattern); [Careers360 2026 Part B paper](https://design.careers360.com/articles/ceed-2026-part-b-question-paper-with-solution); [PW 2027 pattern](https://www.pw.live/design-architecture/exams/ceed-exam-pattern-2027). Caveat: these are coaching sites, not the IIT Bombay brochure. They conflict on the Part A/Part B score weighting (25/75 vs Part A used only to shortlist).

## 1. Known timed ideation drills: mechanics, duration, output, what they train

### Takeaway
The proven drills share one structure: a hard time box, a fixed number of empty slots, a ban on editing, then a separate selection step. They train **fluency** (how many ideas), **flexibility** (how different the ideas are), and **committing under time pressure**, not drawing skill. Card decks (Design Heuristics, Oblique Strategies, IDEO Method Cards) add a different mechanic: a random external prompt that pushes you into another part of the solution space.

### Cited Findings
**Crazy 8s (Google Ventures Design Sprint)**
- Fold a sheet into 8 panels and sketch one idea per panel, about 1 minute each, 8 minutes total — [Deep Impact](https://www.deep-impact.ch/en/insights/crazy-8s-design-sprint); [Conceptboard](https://conceptboard.com/blog/crazy-8s-brainstorming-template/)
- Popularised by Jake Knapp at Google Ventures. Panels can hold sketches, wireframes, or written notes. It is designed to work for non-designers too — [Squarespace blog](https://semicircle-hexagon-kwym.squarespace.com/blog-2-1/the-crazy-8-technique-for-ideating-within-8-minutes); [Bootcamp/uxdesign.cc](https://bootcamp.uxdesign.cc/what-you-need-to-know-about-crazy-8s-7f42851d5c3c); [UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/generate-crazy-ideas-with-this-design-sprint-method-c6a36a16c3d5)
- Variants: some teams run several rounds. "Design Studio" adds a 1-minute pitch per person after the sketching — [Faster Product](https://fasterproduct.substack.com/p/how-to-generate-brilliant-ideas-with-the-design-studio-brainstorming-exercise); [UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/generate-crazy-ideas-with-this-design-sprint-method-c6a36a16c3d5)
- Sources disagree on timing: one 8-minute block vs eight 1-minute blocks with a cue at each minute — [Conceptboard](https://conceptboard.com/blog/crazy-8s-brainstorming-template/)

**30 Circles (Bob McKim → David & Tom Kelley, *Creative Confidence*, 2013)**
- Setup: a sheet of 30 blank circles and 3 minutes. Turn as many circles as possible into recognisable objects. Most people do not finish all 30 — [IDEO](https://www.ideo.com/journal/build-your-creative-confidence-30-circles-exercise)
- The debrief is built in:
  - **Quantity:** how many circles did you fill?
  - **Diversity:** variations on one theme (ball, ball, ball) vs distinct ideas (planet, cookie, face)?
  - **Rule-breaking:** did anyone combine circles?
  - **Assumptions:** did you assume rules that were never stated?

  It trains **fluency vs flexibility**: many variants of one idea give you only one real option — [IDEO](https://www.ideo.com/journal/build-your-creative-confidence-30-circles-exercise)
- Its stated aim is to stop self-censoring — [IDEO 10 exercises](https://www.ideo.com/blog/10-exercises-to-build-your-creative-confidence); [Diana Rendina](https://dianalrendina.substack.com/p/the-30-circles-exercise)

**Thumbnail sketching / 100 ideas**
- LUMA's guidance: time-box each round, don't edit early, and value visual thinking over polished drawings — [LUMA Institute](https://www.luma-institute.com/thumbnail-sketching/)
- An IB design lesson allows 2 minutes per thumbnail. One course has students draw thumbnail frames in proportion to the final art, then pick the strongest 3 before refining — [allrounder.ai IB lesson](https://allrounder.ai/ib-grade-8-product-design/unit-3-idea-generation-concept-development/thumbnail-sketches-translating-ideas-to-form-3-lesson-6847fa); [CityTech COMD3313](https://openlab.citytech.cuny.edu/comd3313sp25/2025/02/07/assignment-1-part-2)
- "100 ideas before picking any" appears as a practitioner discipline, e.g. Proko's Weekly Thumbnail Challenge community — [Proko](https://www.proko.com/community/topics/weekly-thumbnail-challenge). No single canonical 100-thumbnail protocol was found.

**"How might we" (HMW) statements (IDEO, d.school)**
- IDEO's Field Guide: if the challenge is too narrow there is no room to be creative; if too broad there is nowhere to start. Tim Brown: every IDEO challenge begins with HMW, "navigating between the overly general and too specific" — [Greenbook](https://greenbook.org/insights/research-methodologies/framing-your-insights-with-a-8220how-might-we8221-statement-part-2); [Wade Institute](https://studio.wadeinstitute.org.au/tool-detail-how-might-we.html)
- Practical scoping test from third-party guides: a good HMW should yield at least 10 meaningfully different answers. 1–2 answers means too narrow; no starting point means too broad. Example: "redesign travel" is too broad; "redesign an airline's safety speech" is well scoped — [WA gov CX](https://your.wa.gov/cx-resources/how-might-we-statements/); [Atomic Object](https://spin.atomicobject.com/?p=147884)
- Abstraction ladder: ask "why?" if the HMW is too concrete, "what/how?" if it is too abstract — [Slideshare (IDEO/d.school derived)](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/creative-question/43317304)

**Design Heuristics cards (Yilmaz, Daly, Seifert et al.)**
- 77 heuristics, derived from studying innovative products and how industrial and engineering designers ideate. Each card has a prompt, a graphic, and two product examples — [Design Society ICED 2011](https://www.designsociety.org/publication/30741/collaborative_idea_generation_using_design_heuristics); [IJEE 2012](https://www.ijee.ie/articles/Vol28-2/29_ijee2559ns.pdf)
- Evidence: with 48 first-year engineering students, concepts made with the heuristics were more developed and more creative. Some students struggled to apply them — [IJEE](https://www.ijee.ie/articles/Vol28-2/29_ijee2559ns.pdf); [ASEE](https://peer.asee.org/design-heuristics-an-evidence-based-tool-to-improve-innovation)
- In team workshops, one heuristic often moved designers to a different part of the design space and set off other, unprompted heuristics — [Design Society](https://www.designsociety.org/publication/30741/collaborative_idea_generation_using_design_heuristics)

**Oblique Strategies (Eno & Schmidt, 1975)**
- Each card gives a question, dilemma, or new angle of attack, used to get unstuck. The first edition had about 113 cards; later editions vary (a V&A-listed pack has 126); there was a 2001 reissue — [Wikiwand](https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Oblique_Strategies); [Enoshop](https://www.enoshop.co.uk/product/oblique-strategies); [Cool Tools](https://kk.org/cooltools/?p=8)

**IDEO Method Cards (2002/2003)**
- 51 cards in 4 suits: **Learn** (analyse), **Look** (observe), **Ask** (enlist people), **Try** (simulate/prototype). Also released as an iOS app — [IDEO](https://www.ideo.com/post/method-cards); [Cool Tools](https://kk.org/cooltools/ideo-method-car/); [Managing Design blog](https://managingdesignanddevelopment.blogspot.com/2020/01)

### Inferences
- How each drill maps to a CEED question:

  | Drill | CEED question | What it gives her |
  |---|---|---|
  | Crazy 8s | Form from a primitive; icon set | 8 directions in 8 minutes, then commit to one |
  | 30 Circles | Primitive → form | Divergent uses of one shape; trains flexibility |
  | Thumbnails | Unusual-POV scene; 4-frame story | Viewpoint and composition choices before rendering |
  | HMW scoping test | Problem identification | Turns raw observations into well-scoped statements |
  | Design Heuristics | Form/product question | Ready-made transformations to push variety |

- For the HMW question specifically, the "≥10 answers" test can be applied without any drawing.
- Design Heuristics are the most directly transferable card set for form and product variation. Oblique Strategies are better as an "unstick" card mid-attempt.
- IDEO Method Cards fit research and observation (problem identification) more than timed generation.

### Gaps
- No primary sources found for sketchnoting drills, design charrettes, observation journals, or storyboarding drills as named timed protocols. They were not researched in depth within the tool budget.
- The official GV Sprint book wording for Crazy 8s was not fetched; descriptions above are secondary.

## 2. Apps and sites for prompt generation and timed creative practice: what keeps people practising

### Takeaway
Retention comes from four mechanics: **combinatorial randomisation** (Sharpen), **fixed cadence plus a numbered finite run** (Daily UI's 100 weekday emails), **progressive session schedules** (Line of Action / Quickposes class mode), and **fixed-size challenges** (20 images). Drawabox adds a balance rule (half study, half free play) against burnout. Evidence on streaks specifically was not found.

### Cited Findings
- **Sharpen.design:**
  - Pick a difficulty, then get a random prompt that combines two elements (a product need plus a user/context), with an optional timer.
  - 21 categories, 2 generators, and over 15M combinations (11.3M in 2019). Launched on Product Hunt in Feb 2017 by Anthony Gibson and Arman Nobari — [UIUX Showcase](https://uiuxshowcase.com/resources/sharpen-design-challenge-generator/); [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/sharpen/sharpen-design-generator); [Medium/Sharpen](https://medium.com/sharpen-design/overhauling-sharpens-categories-design-prompts-d87ee7d0c96a)
- **Daily UI:**
  - A bare one-line prompt is emailed every weekday for 100 days. Prompts are numbered (#1 Sign Up … #100 Redesign Daily UI).
  - Participants add their own time limits (e.g. design within 1 hour of opening the email).
  - Users criticised the emails for having no examples — [Medium](https://medium.com/@dmtors/my-4-5-month-journey-with-daily-ui-1ce0f103d2bb); [Prototypr](https://blog.prototypr.io/what-i-wish-i-knew-before-the-dailyui-challenge-da4c3fa97e8c); [UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/i-did-the-daily-ui-challenge-for-15-days-and-this-is-what-i-learnt-3ed0ca8f0473)
- **Line of Action:**
  - "Class mode" is a site-set schedule that lengthens each pose over the session, starting with forced short ones. Sessions run 30 min to 6 hrs.
  - "Standard mode" lets you set a custom interval and pose count.
  - Users say class mode builds speed and looseness but suits some subjects (gesture) better than others (portraits) — [Line of Action forum](https://www.line-of-action.com/forums/topic/benefits-of-standard-mode-vs-class-mode); [Claudia Bliss](https://claudiabliss-newsletter.beehiiv.com/p/mastering-the-art-of-gesture-drawing); [LoA tools update](https://line-of-action.com/article/a-new-version-of-the-tools-is-now-online)
- **Quickposes:**
  - Separate modes: "Random" (no timer) and "Timed practice." Challenges are a fixed 20 images.
  - Users cite Quickposes' increasing-timer class mode as the model they want in other tools — [Quickposes challenges](https://quickposes.com/gestures/challenges); [Proko forum](https://proko.com/community/topics/timer-tool-improvements)
  - A Proko user asks for "show the pictures from the session at the end," i.e. a post-session review grid — [Proko forum](https://www.proko.com/community/topics/very-importan-missing-feature-on-the-timer-tool-show-the-pictures-shown-in-the-timed-session-at-the-end)
- **Drawabox:**
  - The "50% rule": spend as much time on free "play" drawing as on exercises. Its stated purpose is sustainability, not improving quality.
  - The 250 Box Challenge is framed as a marathon to pace out, not a sprint — [Drawabox community](https://drawabox.com/community/submission/5DBBE4UI); [Drawabox r/ArtFundamentals](https://drawabox.com/r/artfundamentals/post/10n7lyc); [Chris Padilla](https://chrisdpadilla-com.vercel.app/250boxchallengecomplete)

### Inferences
- Copy the class-mode idea, applied to *thinking* time rather than pose time: start with forced-short ideation rounds (e.g. 3 min), then lengthen toward the full 24-minute exam block.
- Sharpen's two-slot structure maps directly onto CEED templates: [primitive] × [user/context], or [symbol set] × [narrative twist].
- An end-of-session review grid of all attempts (the Proko request) is a cheap, high-value feature. With photo capture, it becomes the review surface.

### Gaps
- No NID/UCEED/CEED-specific prompt-bank apps were verified. Inktober and Sketch-a-day mechanics were not researched.
- No research found on whether streaks help or hurt creative practice. This needs a separate search.

## 3. Tool mechanics for thinking under time

### Takeaway
The research supports four mechanics:
- **Phased timers with a forced commit point** — separate generating ideas from judging them.
- **Idea-count targets with a diversity check** — not count alone.
- **Lens/heuristic prompts injected mid-session** — move her to new parts of the solution space.
- **No exposure to example solutions before she ideates** — prevents fixation.

### Cited Findings
- Separating generation from judgement: "Quantity before judgement" and "refrain from editing early" in thumbnail rounds; then pick the top 3 to refine — [LUMA](https://www.luma-institute.com/thumbnail-sketching/); [CityTech](https://openlab.citytech.cuny.edu/comd3313sp25/2025/02/07/assignment-1-part-2)
- Count is not enough: in 30 Circles, many variants of one theme count as one idea. The debrief explicitly checks distinctness — [IDEO](https://www.ideo.com/journal/build-your-creative-confidence-30-circles-exercise)
- **Design fixation** (Jansson & Smith 1991): seeing a picture of an example solution reduced the number of ideas and the number of idea categories, and the example's features were copied. This held for professional designers too, and in the original study even when people were told not to copy. A later replication found explicit "don't use the example" instructions removed fixation, so the effect of instructions is contested — [Crilly 2019 (core.ac.uk)](https://core.ac.uk/download/158355214.pdf); [core.ac.uk replication](https://core.ac.uk/download/237398528.pdf)
- Pictures vs words: fixation can come from both pictorial and verbal examples; modality and familiarity are studied variables — [Design Society](https://www.designsociety.org/download-publication/32263/idea_fixation_in_design_the_in_uence_of_pictures_and_words); [AI EDAM](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/ai-edam/article/study-on-the-effects-of-example-familiarity-and-modality-on-design-fixation/AD93E342571AB85057D36CFD4C53EE77)
- Heuristic prompts redirect search: a single Design Heuristic card moved teams into a different region of the design space — [Design Society](https://www.designsociety.org/publication/30741/collaborative_idea_generation_using_design_heuristics)

### Inferences
- Suggested phase timer for a 24-minute block: read/decode ~2 → diverge (thumbnails, target count) ~6 → **commit (forced single choice, typed one-line rationale)** ~1 → execute ~13 → buffer ~2. This is a synthesis, not a sourced protocol. Tune it per question type, e.g. HMW needs a longer diverge phase and less execution.
- Do not show reference solutions or past toppers' answers before an attempt (fixation risk). Show them only after the attempt, in review.
- Diversity check without judging drawings: after the diverge phase, she types one tag per thumbnail (e.g. "fold," "stack," "split"). The app counts **distinct** tags, not total thumbnails. This applies the 30 Circles flexibility metric.
- Spaced re-attempts: re-serve the same prompt days later and compare the tag sets. Growth shows up as new categories, not better drawings. (No direct source on spaced re-attempts for ideation; see gap.)
- Photo capture is mainly for the review grid and self-comparison. The app never scores the image.

### Gaps
- No studies found on spaced repetition or re-attempts for *ideation* skill specifically, or on post-attempt reflection prompts in timed design exams.
- No empirical source for optimal phase splits within a 24-minute design question.

## 4. LLMs as a text-only sparring partner: evidence and design implications

### Takeaway
Evidence from 2024–2025 is consistent on two points:
- **Individuals:** LLM help raises how many ideas people produce (fluency) and how good each idea is, especially for weaker performers.
- **The idea pool:** it shrinks diversity across people, lowers ownership, and — when used *before* own ideation — cuts original ideas and creative self-efficacy.

The safe role for an LLM is **after** she ideates: a provocateur and critic over her typed notes, plus a prompt generator. It should not be an idea source during divergence.

### Cited Findings
- **Meincke, Nave, Terwiesch, *Nature Human Behaviour* 2025:** across 5 experiments, ChatGPT-assisted brainstorming gave narrower idea sets, significant in 37 of 45 comparisons. In one task, 94% of ChatGPT-assisted ideas overlapped and 9 participants independently named their toy "Build-a-Breeze Castle." Human ideas were all unique — [Wharton Mack Institute](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/2025/new-in-nature-chatgpt-decreases-idea-diversity-in-brainstorming/)
- **Doshi & Hauser, *Science Advances* 2024 (~300 writers, GPT-4):** AI ideas made stories more creative, better written, and more enjoyable, mostly for less creative writers, with no gain for highly creative ones. Similarity between AI-assisted stories rose by 10.7%. Novelty +8.1% and usefulness +9% for the most-access group — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532); [Exeter](https://news.exeter.ac.uk/?p=13749)
- **Anderson, Shah, Kreminski, C&C 2024 (N=36):** ChatGPT users produced more ideas and more detailed ideas, but less semantically distinct ones across users, and they felt less responsible for their ideas — [arXiv 2402.01536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01536)
- **Qin et al., "Timing Matters," CHI 2025 (N=60):** using an LLM from the start, rather than after independent ideation, reduced the number of original ideas and lowered creative self-efficacy and self-credit. Autonomy and ownership mediated the effect. The authors recommend delaying LLM use — [arXiv 2502.06197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06197)
- **LLMs fixate too:** ChatGPT-4o produced more ideas than humans on the egg task, but most fell into conventional categories, i.e. similar fixation — [Frontiers in Psychology 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12369561)
- **Prompting raises LLM output diversity:** human-group ideas had cosine similarity ≈0.243 vs 0.255–0.432 for GPT-4 (lower = more diverse). Chain-of-thought ("generate 100, then pick the most distinct") came closest to human groups. A persona prompt helped little (0.368 vs 0.377 baseline) — [Meincke, Mollick, Terwiesch, SSRN 2024](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4708466); [Mack Institute PDF](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/for-web-AI-idea-variance.pdf)
- **Provocations rather than answers:** in AI-assisted knowledge work, AI-generated "provocations" nudged people toward critical and metacognitive thinking (many effects not statistically significant). Actionable provocations were valued over vague critique — [Drosos, Sarkar et al., arXiv 2501.17247](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17247v1)
- **LLM devil's advocate (CHI '25 LBW):** surfaced overlooked options and countered comfortable agreement in group decisions — [arXiv 2503.14263](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2503.14263). A CHI '26 paper argues provocations should adapt to the user's expertise and require user contribution ("productive friction") — [arXiv 2603.19975](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.19975.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Role ordering:** LLM off during read/diverge/commit. On only for (a) generating the prompt before the attempt and (b) the debrief after it. This follows the Timing Matters and Jansson & Smith findings.
- **Prompt generator:** use a generate-many-then-select-distinct prompt (CoT style) plus a log of past prompts to avoid repeats and homogeneous prompt sets. Templates per CEED type: POV × scene, symbol set, icon theme, primitive × function, Indian everyday setting.
- **Devil's advocate on her chosen idea (text only):** ask her for a one-line rationale at the commit point. The LLM then returns 2–3 *actionable* challenges, e.g. "who is the user?", "what breaks at scale?", "why is this POV unusual?". It does not suggest replacement ideas.
- **Cliché detector:** after she types her thumbnail tags, the LLM flags which are the most common or default responses for that prompt. This mirrors the egg-task finding that the conventional categories are predictable. Framing: "this is the first thing most people think of" — not a score.
- **Debrief from typed notes:** reflection questions (what did you reject and why, where did time go, what would you change). No grading of drawings, which preserves ownership.
- **Risk to keep visible:** any LLM-suggested idea she adopts pushes her toward the same ideas the LLM gives everyone else. In a ranked exam, distinctiveness matters, so in-ideation suggestions are a net negative even if per-idea quality rises.

### Gaps
- No study found specifically on LLM support for timed *sketch-based* design exams, or on Indian design-entrance prep.
- Full texts of the Nature Human Behaviour paper (mitigation conditions) and the Cognitive Science Society GPT-4 sketch-inspiration study ([eScholarship](https://journalpub.escholarship.org/cognitivesciencesociety/article/49948/galley/37910/download)) were not read. Their outcomes are unconfirmed here.
- A claimed meta-analysis of 19 studies on AI homogenisation was seen only on a low-quality aggregator and is not reported.
